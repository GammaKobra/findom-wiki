In 30 seconds

  • Every claim carries one of five badges: Verified, Reported, Community knowledge, Vivian’s field notes or Unverified.
  • Verified and Reported claims link to a source that was opened and read, with the date it was read and an archived copy.
  • Field notes are experience, written as experience, and never dressed up as a study.
  • A badge tells you where a claim comes from. It does not tell you what to do.
  • Where the evidence is thin, the wiki says so instead of filling the gap.

The five levels

A badge sits right after the sentence it backs, and one badge backs one claim. Each claim has its own number on the page. At the end of every chapter, the “Check it” box lists the claims with their level, the title of the source, a link, a link to an archived copy and the date the source was read.

What Verified covers. The badge covers the source. A platform’s terms prove what the platform states, and a study proves what its authors found in their sample. Neither proves more than that. Reported sits below Verified because a journalist stands between you and the source.

What field notes are. They are my account of the scene, gathered from experience and from people inside it, written in general terms with no names and no anecdotes that could be recognised. A quantity from experience is written as an estimate and never as a statistic.

What a source must pass

A source does not back a claim because it exists. It backs a claim after these steps.

From page to badge

  1. 1

    A writer opens the source and reads it. A page found by search and not opened is recorded as found, not read, and it backs nothing.

  2. 2

    The writer records the claim and a quote of at most fifteen words, copied from the page.

  3. 3

    A tool downloads the page itself, turns it into plain text and looks for each recorded quote, ignoring case, punctuation and spacing. This step exists because the research tools used by writers can return a summary of a page, and a summary can change the words. Each quote is marked verbatim, not found or fetch failed. Pages that only display in a browser are opened in a headless browser.

  4. 4

    The evidence check lets a Verified or Reported marker cite a source only when its status is read and at least one of its quotes was found word for word.

  5. 5

    Every outside link gets an archived copy. If a page disappears, the chapter points to the archive.

Sources that were found but never read are listed apart on the Sources page, and they back nothing. A quote that matches a page proves that the page says it. It does not prove that the page is right, which is why the badge also says what kind of source it is.

The checks before release

Six checks run before release, and the code run applies to the chapters that contain code. They were written for this wiki and they live in its tools folder.

Six checks

  1. 1

    Evidence check

    It reads every page and stops on a marker that is unknown or badly formed. It stops on a Verified or Reported marker with no source behind it, a source that was not read, a Community marker with fewer than two sources, and a quote that was not found word for word. It also stops on a link to a chapter that does not exist, a dictionary word with no entry, a long dash, an exclamation mark and a contraction.

  2. 2

    Warnings

    The same check warns about a number, an amount or a percentage in a paragraph with no marker, a paragraph of the body with no marker, and a sentence of more than forty words. It also warns about first-person plural pronouns, because the wiki speaks as Vivian or as plain reference. A warning does not stop a chapter, and each one is read by a person.

  3. 3

    Code run

    Every code example is run for real, in a throwaway copy, with a time limit. The output on the page is the output the code produced. A chapter fails if an example has no real output, or if its files changed after the run. An example that reaches the network or starts other programs is refused.

  4. 4

    Link check

    Every outside link and every source address is requested, and an archived copy is looked up for each one. The check only reads public pages, and it never asks the archive to save anything. A dead link with no archive copy blocks release.

  5. 5

    Metadata check

    Every PDF and every image is searched for personal information, such as author fields, hidden metadata packets, camera data, folder paths, drive paths and e-mail addresses. A second detector runs after it. Nothing ships with a name inside.

  6. 6

    Field-note review

    Every field note is listed on a sheet with three boxes: true, safe to publish, and without risk. A note is cleared only when all three are ticked, and a release is blocked while any note is not cleared.

The other rules

  • A figure from a source never appears without a link, a date and the sentence it came from. “Thousands” counts as an Unverified claim.
  • An estimate from experience is written as an estimate, with the words “in my experience”.
  • A contested claim shows both sides, each with its source.
  • Anything that changes, such as prices, platform rules, laws and help lines, carries the words “as of” and a date.
  • A phone number for help is published only after the official site of the service was opened, and it carries the date.
  • Health, money and law chapters point to the official service of the reader’s country. They give landmarks, not a verdict on your case.
  • Nothing is copied from another author. Studies stay as links because their licences do not allow hosting them, and the only PDFs offered are those written here.

  • Myth

    A badge means the claim is true.

    Fact

    A badge shows where a claim comes from and how far it was checked. Even a Verified claim is only as good as its source.

  • Myth

    A claim with no source is worthless.

    Fact

    A field note is worth what experience is worth: useful to start from, and not a measurement. It says so on its badge.

  • Myth

    If the checks pass, the chapter is right.

    Fact

    The checks catch missing sources, broken links, unreal code output and leaked metadata. They cannot catch a source that is mistaken, which is why claims carry dates and why you should read the source yourself.
