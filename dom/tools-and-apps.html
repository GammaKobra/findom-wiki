The kinds of tools and what they’re for

A persona uses a handful of kinds of tools, and most of them are small, ordinary software used by an audience that agreed to it. The ones that need the most care touch money, accounts or another person’s computer. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

This chapter describes kinds and names no tool brands, because brands and plans change within weeks. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Tools made by Vivian appear as examples and are marked as hers. They aren’t adverts and they carry no buying link.

Making images, edits, video and sound

Generators. Mainstream image and chat assistants often refuse adult-leaning edits, or retry in a loop and burn through your allowance. Read each provider’s terms before you upload anything, and be ready to finish an image by hand in an ordinary editor. Your hardware decides what runs on your own computer: a strong graphics card gives you images in seconds, a weak one takes minutes per picture. A provider can also delete a conversation about a project it doesn’t like, so keep copies of your prompts somewhere you control. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Editors. The 2DFD editor, made by Vivian, is a free editor for findom posts, with censor styles, captions, cut-outs and hypno loops, and it works offline on your own computer. It’s designed to refuse an export that flashes too fast and to strip hidden data from files before they leave. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. That’s what it’s designed to do. It doesn’t certify that it always does it.

A photo or a video can carry hidden data about where and when it was made, so clean every file before you post it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Stay anonymous.

Flashing. A spiral or a loop can hurt the person watching it. The web accessibility guidelines say a page must not flash more than three times in any one-second period, unless the flash is below the stated thresholds. The guidance behind them says flashing content can cause migraines, dizziness, nausea and seizures. VerifiedChecked against the original source.Understanding Success Criterion 2.3.1: Three Flashes or Below Threshold | WAI | W3C (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026. Devices let people ask for reduced motion, and a page can detect that request. ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.prefers-reduced-motion CSS media feature - CSS | MDN (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026. Count the flashes in anything you make, warn before it plays and offer a calm version. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Hypno and edits.

Licences. A tool you give away may contain parts other people made: a cut-out model, a font, a sound. Check the licence of every part and ship each licence with its part. Cloning the voice of a real or licensed character in a tool you sell is a rights problem. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Art for your persona.

Where people find you: pages, bots and posts

A hub page replaces a link-in-bio page. It says who the persona is, what she offers and how to join, and it shows a free teaser. It saves you from repeating the same answers in every message, but you still have to talk to people. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Test it the way a visitor sees it: on a computer and on more than one phone, because the same layout can look different from one device to the next. Heavy effects make phones lag, banners crop differently on a phone, and cards must scroll wherever you touch them. Show the page to someone outside before launch, because the person who made it stops seeing small oddities. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Visitor privacy. Fonts and scripts loaded from other companies let those companies see every visitor. If you serve everything from your own hosting, only the host sees a visit. An 18+ door at the entrance tells visitors what the site is. A click-through door doesn’t check anyone’s age, so word it honestly. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Some places require real age checks for adult sites, so check the rule where you and your visitors live, or ask a lawyer. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

Domain and continuity. A static site can be hosted for free, and the domain, in my experience about USD 10.00 per year, is the only recurring cost. A new adult-themed domain may get blocked by a safe-browsing filter soon after launch, so test early and keep a backup name. A free backend tier may pause a project nobody uses and take the pages that depend on it down with it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Bots. A bot in your chat server can run missions: you create one, a member chooses to accept it and hands it in privately, and you validate or refuse it. Rewards are badges, roles and points. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Discord treats sexually explicit content as age-restricted and meant for users who are 18 or older, and it keeps that content out of open areas such as profile surfaces. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A tool I would accept

  1. 1

    It arrives off

    Nothing runs until you start it, and the gentlest level is the default. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    It does one named thing on one named site

    It tells you what it will touch before it touches it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  3. 3

    It counts confirmed payments only

    A pending or unknown payment never counts as a success. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  4. 4

    It shows a running total

    The total stays visible. It’s never hidden or rounded away. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  5. 5

    It enforces a limit set in advance

    You type the limit while you’re calm, and a server or your bank holds it, never the page. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  6. 6

    The stop works in every state

    Idle, running, locked, and just after a payment. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  7. 7

    It leaves in one click

    Removing it takes one action, and it keeps nothing about you that it doesn’t need. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The list below names them so you can spot them in a tool you’re offered or about to order. The answer is always the same: the limit you set first, the tab you close and the bank statement you check.

Designs that push spending
What they say or doThe counter
A hidden or random total, or a price that changesYou can’t judge what you spend. Ask for the number first, and leave if there isn’t one.
A pay-more mode that sells better oddsYou pay more for a chance, and the house still wins. Keep your limit whatever the mode is called.
A mascot that sulks, or a thank-you clip that invites another turnIt’s a script timed to make you go again. A face doesn’t move your limit.
A countdown or timer on the purchase prompt, or a lock that removes the stopA countdown rushes you to commit before you think, and a stop that can be removed is no stop. Refuse a tool that rushes you. A wait before raising a limit protects you.
A public tally, a leaderboard or a prize for the first sendersA ranking turns spending into a contest. Don’t compare yourself with anybody.
A mode that runs overnight, aims at payday or sets a deadlineIt goes for the hours when your choices are worst. Refuse any tool that picks its own moment.
A near miss, a paid exit or a penalty for breaking a streakA near miss feels like a win you almost had, a paid exit charges you to leave and a streak penalty punishes you for resting. Ending the session costs nothing, so end it.

A careful maker can leave every one of these out. Ask whether they did.

Remote-action features are the dangerous end. A hub that can make accounts follow, post or send, send files or reach a terminal gives its maker control of every installed copy, and anyone who finds a flaw gets the same control. If you find such a flaw, tell the maker and don’t use it. Remote control of a sub’s computer or accounts is never part of the game. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. It’s also a route for scams, and a US government alert advises people not to give control of their computers or mobile devices to any stranger. Working with a maker, or becoming one

Most doms can’t code, so tools come from makers, and many makers steer AI assistants instead of writing the code by hand. An assistant can go too far and break parts that worked, so retest the whole tool after every change. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A split that works: the maker builds, tests and records, and you review every line of text and sound and say exactly what to change. Ask for a numbered build with a plain readme, a screen recording of every new mode, and a test run signed out of every account, on a phone and on a computer. A payment path that works doesn’t prove the tool is ready, because the rest of it can still be broken. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

VVivian’s field notes

Making images, code or edits for a dom is fair only when the sub chose the scope, can refuse without losing his place, and gets thanked. Otherwise it’s free labour dressed up as devotion. I say it so nobody codes for praise and calls it kink.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Settle in writing who holds the domain, the hosting and the keys, and who can change or share the tool. Keys and tokens are typed only into a masked prompt in the owner’s own terminal. They never go in a chat, a commit, a screenshot or a document. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Copying. In my experience ideas get copied within weeks, and what draws people is the account that promotes a tool, more than its code. Study other makers and copy their ideas freely, but leave their code, assets and wording alone, and never answer a rival with reports. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Burnout. A maker who starts too many projects ends up with unfinished things everywhere. List them all, finish what’s well under way, and work on one at a time. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
