Becoming a dom Chapter 1

Build a persona

Last checked 3 November 20269 minutes to read

A persona is a character with a rulebook. This chapter shows how to write the rules, the voice and the look down before the first post, and where persona design starts to pull on the people it serves.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

In 30 seconds

  • A persona is a written file: name, tone, marks, voice rules, look and limits.
  • The limits and the wall between character and person are written first, before any money moves.
  • The authority is performed. Harshness is a role, and it can be on request only.
  • Entry rules, prices and ranks are persuasion. Say every condition before the first send.
  • Pace matters: a persona that depends on one tired person is fragile.

You will learn

  • What goes into a persona file, and in what order to write it.
  • How a voice and a look stay recognisable.
  • Which limits to write down, and why they are written and not remembered.
  • How entry rules and ranks look from the sub’s chair.
  • How to keep the pace without burning out.

A persona is a costume with a rulebook. I wrote mine down before anyone paid me anything, and that is why it holds on a tired evening. Write yours first, and let the file decide what happens at night, not the mood.

The persona file

Write the persona into one file before you post anything. The file makes the character repeatable across a site, posts, chat and tools, and it makes you consistent when you are tired. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What goes in the file

  1. 1

    Name and tone

    A name, one sentence of tone and a short bio. Vivian is cold, possessive and never vulgar. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    Marks

    A mark of the universe such as a seal or a colour, a sign-off, a welcome line for returning visitors and a line that says play has manners. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  3. 3

    House voice

    Fixed rules for how she writes, listed below. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  4. 4

    Limits

    What she never does, written in full. See Limits and the wall, below. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  5. 5

    Look recipe

    The few features that make her recognisable, written so anyone can reproduce them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Vivian’s voice rules are short enough to learn by heart. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Two short sentences, the second turned toward him.
  • One praise word at most per screen.
  • She never shouts, and exclamation marks and emoji stay out.
  • Insults and threats stay out.
  • Never vague about money.

The voice reaches the buttons. A generic label such as “pay now” felt wrong in her mouth, so the wording was drafted in voice first: plain and confident, never cute. All public text is in one language, and a stray line in another language reads as a leak. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

New doms usually learn a voice by imitating someone, and that is fine for a while. The risk is sounding like a copy, so let the voice drift toward the character you are building. Taste in writing is part of identity: choose and defend your lines, and do not hand the voice over wholesale to a machine. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Look and signs

A persona is recognised at thumbnail size, so write the look as a recipe: hair, eye colour, ears, one accessory, and always an adult. Consistency of the recipe matters more than any single image. One signature look repeated across content is easier to recognise than many looks, and a seasonal look works best as a layer on top that can be switched on and off. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The style has to fit the dominance the persona performs. Soft, cute styles read as sweet, and a commanding register usually needs a more mature look. The owner lives with the style every day, so the owner’s taste decides. A friend’s taste is an opinion. If the images come from another person, agree in writing who owns them and who is credited before they spread. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Many personas are built on an existing character, and the studio that made the design keeps it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Art for your persona.

Small repeating signs make a world. A signature phrase at the end of a session, stickers and emotes made from the character, short dance loops, one colour and a small vocabulary of file numbers and stamps all do it. A lore frame such as a court or a cult makes the calls to action easy to write. It can also make a send feel like loyalty, so read it as theatre, with the cap as the only ceiling. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Limits and the wall

Limits live in the file and not only in your head. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Vivian’s file says what she never does.

  • Meet in real life.
  • Use a camera.
  • Use her voice.
  • Appear as a minor or as anything child-like.
  • Do scat or gore.
  • Chase anyone.
  • Cross a sub’s real limits.

The hardest line is the first one. Anything that touches real life is a no, and talk of meeting is refused. Never confirm a real-life detail about the person behind the persona, and keep the story closed. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. That wall is what protects both sides. See Stay anonymous.

Never chase. The sub comes to the persona, and she does not follow up. This keeps the relationship voluntary and removes the pressure that follow-ups create. When someone asks you to stop messaging, stop. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Harshness is a role. A dom with a gentle nature can find a cruel tone hard to play, because it can feel like real cruelty. The answer is that it is performance, many subs ask for it, and you return to yourself afterwards. A persona can be soft by default and sharp only on request. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Rules come before money. A first sub should meet the frame before the first sendsendA payment from a sub to a dom, often by buying a wishlist item. Informal forms are sends and sendies. Press calls a payment made without a session a silent tribute. A send buys nothing, and in practice it cannot be refunded.: what the persona keeps, what she throws out, her limits and how to stop. A list of what she throws out belongs to the act. Keep it about behaviour, such as haggling or wasting time, and never about a sub’s money troubles. The person who is paid cannot be the safety net. If a sub says money is short, the money part stops for that evening. If he says he is in trouble, you stop, listen, ask for nothing more and point outward: emergency services, a helpline, someone physically near him. Entry rules and pressure designs

A persona is also a funnel, and a funnel is persuasion. A persona that is warm in voice but hard about money is still hard about money. Safe design often means earning less. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Some doms ask for a small fixed send to get in. In my experience it draws many people and many leave quickly, while adding conditions such as a session or two draws fewer people who stay longer. Some keep the public step low and explain further conditions in private. I would state every condition before the first send, because a condition that appears after the money moved is a pressure design. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

On time, some doms cap chat with contacts who never send at about an hour a day, and others ignore unknown messages. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Both are choices about time, and neither changes how fast a sub can stop.

Pace, change and burnout

Domming is mostly written work, seven days a week and always on. Solo builders run the persona, the tools and the community together, and the advice that holds up is plain. Pick one to three things for a day. Finish what is past roughly ten percent before starting something new. Stop measuring yourself against other doms. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Burnout shows as anxiety every time you open the project and a feeling that nothing works. The response is to step back: a few days to a week of not posting, while still answering existing subs. Taking a step back is often right, and you can return. A dom who disappears into building loses trust, because subs need regular contact. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A persona stays composed in front of subs. Keep your private life out of sessions, and keep at least one real person you can talk to, because smiling through pain with nobody to tell is not a plan. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Personas drift. One may change theme, which means the bio, the site and the posts have to be rewritten together, and a hook that no longer fits should be dropped. Looks drift as generators change, so keep old and new images in separate folders and decide case by case which belong to the current look. Studying other personas is useful. Study them, ask before you copy anything, and then differ. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
