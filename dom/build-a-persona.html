Step 1 of 6 What a persona is

In 30 seconds

  • A persona is a written file: name, tone, marks, voice rules, look and limits.
  • Write the limits, and the wall between the character and you, first, before any money moves.
  • The authority is an act. Harshness is a role, and it can be on request only.
  • Entry rules, prices and ranks are persuasion. Say every condition before the first send.
  • Pace matters: a persona that runs on one tired person breaks easily.

You will learn

  • What goes in a persona file, and in what order to write it.
  • How to keep a voice and a look recognisable.
  • Which limits to write down, and why you write them instead of remembering them.
  • What entry rules and ranks look like from the sub’s side.
  • How to keep going without burning out.

I wrote mine down before anyone paid me anything, and that’s why it holds when I’m tired. Write yours first, and on a bad night, do what the file says.

The persona file

Write the persona into one file before you post anything. The file lets you repeat the character the same way across a site, posts, chat and tools, and it keeps you consistent when you’re tired. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What goes in the file

  1. 1

    Name and tone

    A name, one sentence on her tone and a short bio. Vivian is cold, possessive and never vulgar. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    Marks

    Something from her world, like a seal or a colour, a sign-off, a welcome line for people who come back, and a line that says play has manners. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  3. 3

    House voice

    Fixed rules for how she writes, listed below. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  4. 4

    Limits

    What she never does, written out in full. See Limits and the wall, below. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  5. 5

    Look recipe

    The few features that make her recognisable, written so anyone can reproduce them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Vivian’s voice rules are short enough to learn by heart. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Two short sentences, the second one turned toward him.
  • One praise word per screen, at most.
  • She never shouts, and she never uses exclamation marks or emoji.
  • She doesn’t insult or threaten.
  • Never vague about money.

The voice reaches the buttons too. A generic label like “pay now” felt wrong coming from her, so the wording was drafted in her voice first: plain and confident, never cute. All public text is in one language, and a stray line in another one reads as a leak. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

New doms usually learn a voice by copying someone, and that’s fine for a while. The risk is sounding like a copy, so let the voice drift toward the character you’re building. Your taste in writing is part of who you are: pick your lines, stand by them, and don’t hand the whole voice over to a machine. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Look and signs

People recognise a persona at thumbnail size, so write the look as a recipe: hair, eye colour, ears, one accessory, and always an adult. Sticking to the recipe matters more than any single image. One signature look repeated everywhere is easier to recognise than lots of looks, and a seasonal look works best as a layer on top that you can switch on and off. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The style has to fit the dominance she plays. Soft, cute styles read as sweet, and a commanding tone usually needs a more mature look. You live with the style every day, so your taste decides. A friend’s taste is just an opinion. If someone else makes the images, agree in writing who owns them and who gets credit before they spread. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Lots of personas are built on an existing character, and the studio that made the design keeps it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Art for your persona.

Small repeating signs build a world: a signature phrase at the end of a session, stickers and emotes of the character, short dance loops, one colour, and a small vocabulary of file numbers and stamps. A lore frame like a court or a cult makes calls to action easy to write. It can also make a send feel like loyalty, so read it as theatre, with the limit as the only ceiling. Limits and the wall

Limits go in the file as well as in your head. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Vivian’s file lists what she never does.

  • Meet in real life.
  • Use a camera.
  • Use her voice.
  • Appear as a minor or as anything child-like.
  • Do scat or gore.
  • Chase anyone.
  • Cross a sub’s real limits.

The hardest line is the first one. Anything that touches real life is a no, and she refuses any talk of meeting. Never confirm a real-life detail about the person behind the persona, and keep the story closed. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. That wall protects both sides. See Stay anonymous.

Never chase. The sub comes to the persona, and she doesn’t follow up. That keeps it voluntary and takes away the pressure follow-ups create. When someone asks you to stop messaging, stop. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Harshness is a role. A dom with a gentle nature can find a cruel tone hard to play, because it can feel like real cruelty. The answer: it’s a performance, lots of subs ask for it, and you go back to being yourself afterwards. A persona can be soft by default and sharp only when asked. Entry rules and pressure designs

A persona is also a funnel, and a funnel is persuasion. If she’s warm in voice but hard about money, she’s still hard about money. Safe design often means earning less. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Some doms ask for a small fixed send to get in. In my experience that brings in lots of people who leave fast, while adding conditions, like a session or two, brings fewer people who stay longer. Some keep the public step low and explain the other conditions in private. I’d state every condition before the first send, because a condition that shows up after the money moved is a pressure design. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

On time, some doms limit chat with people who never send to about an hour a day, and others ignore messages from people they don’t know. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Both are choices about time, and neither changes how fast a sub can stop.

Pace, change and burnout

Domming is mostly writing, seven days a week, always on. If you build alone, you run the persona, the tools and the community at once, and the advice that holds up is plain. Pick one to three things a day. Finish anything that’s past about ten percent before you start something new. Stop measuring yourself against other doms. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Burnout shows up as anxiety every time you open the project and a feeling that nothing works. What helps is stepping back: a few days to a week without posting, while you still answer your existing subs. Stepping back is often the right call, and you can come back. A dom who disappears into building loses trust, because subs need regular contact. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

In front of subs, a persona stays composed. Keep your private life out of sessions, and keep at least one real person you can talk to, because smiling through pain with nobody to tell isn’t a plan. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Personas drift. One might change theme, and then the bio, the site and the posts all need rewriting together, and a hook that no longer fits should go. Looks drift as generators change, so keep old and new images in separate folders and decide case by case which ones belong to the current look. Studying other personas is useful. Study them, ask before you copy anything, then make yours different. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
