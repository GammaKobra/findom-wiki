Becoming a dom Chapter 2

Art for your persona

Last checked 3 November 202610 minutes to read

A drawn persona needs art that you have the right to use. This chapter compares a paid artist, generated images and an existing character, and names the two lines that never move.

VerifiedChecked against the original source.Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

In 30 seconds

  • There are three routes to a look: a paid artist, generated images, an existing character. Each trades cost, control and rights.
  • Every persona is an adult in looks and setting, whatever the lore says. No platform and no rule of this wiki makes an exception.
  • A real person’s face or voice is never used without that person’s consent. Several laws now treat sexual fakes of real adults as crimes.
  • A studio’s character belongs to the studio, and two publishers’ own policies forbid earning money from their material.
  • Check every image before it goes public, and settle credit in writing before art spreads.

You will learn

  • What a paid artist, a generator and an existing character each allow and risk.
  • How platforms treat drawn, generated and realistic adult art, as of 2026-11-03.
  • Which laws cover sexual fakes of real people, in plain words.
  • How to keep a look steady and check an image before it goes out.
  • When making art for a persona is a fair gift and when it is free labour.

Everything a sub knows about me is a picture, and every picture has a maker, a rule and a risk. I chose my look once and I guard it. Before you spend anything on art, know who made it, who may use it, and whether she is an adult.

Three routes to a look

A personapersonaThe character a dom plays. It holds a name, a look, a voice, rules and limits, and it keeps the real person apart. The persona is a performance, and the feelings of the people who meet her are real. needs a look before she needs an audience, and there are three ways to get one. A paid artist draws her. A generator produces her from a written description and reference images. Or she is built on a character that already exists. Most personas mix routes sooner or later, and each route trades cost, control and rights. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The look itself goes into the persona file, as Build a persona explains.

Before a paid artist starts

  1. 1

    Say that the work is adult and for a persona, and get a clear yes before any sketch is made.

  2. 2

    Fix the scope, the credit and the use: how many pieces, where the artist is named, and whether stickers, products or animation are included.

  3. 3

    Ask whether the artist uses generated images in the work, and say whether you accept that.

  4. 4

    Fix the payment, with dates and what happens if either side stops.

  5. 5

    Write it in plain words that both of you can reread, and keep a copy.

That list names topics. It is not contract wording, and this wiki cannot supply wording for your country.

The two lines that never move

Adults only

Every persona is an adult in her looks and in her setting, whatever the lore says. A birthday written in a biography changes nothing about a drawing, so “adult by lore” does not count, and a character who is a student, or who looks like one, is out. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Platform rules name drawn and generated material, not only photographs.

Check each image for child-like proportions, faces and settings, and discard what fails. Do not try to repair it. An adult character also says nothing about the audience, because a drawing cannot check anyone’s age. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

No real person without consent

Never use a real person’s face or voice without that person’s consent. This covers a photo used as a reference, a real name typed into a prompt, and a voice cloned from a performer, even when that voice speaks as a cartoon. Cloning a licensed character’s official voice for something you sell is also a rights risk, and this wiki does not call it standard practice. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A studio’s character is the studio’s

Using a famous character feels free because the tools already know her. She is not free: the studio owns the design. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Publishers say in their own words what fans may do.

  • Microsoft’s game content rules say that an item made with its material cannot be sold or earn any compensation, including through ads, and cannot be pornographic or lewd. VerifiedChecked against the original source.Game Content Usage Rules (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026.
  • Square Enix West says not to use its materials to make money or gain any other financial benefit, and it lists sexually explicit content among what not to make. VerifiedChecked against the original source.Square Enix West Material Usage Policy (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026.

Those two pages speak for two companies only. Other studios have other policies, so read the one that applies. A persona that sells content or earns from pages built on a studio’s character is the case these policies forbid, and nobody has promised a tolerance to rely on. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The business risk follows. A character you do not own can leave your page the day her owner objects, and the account that carried her may go with her. She is also shared with other personas, so the look is never fully yours. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Keeping the look steady

A steady look is the hardest technical problem for a generated persona. The method that holds is short. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A recipe that keeps a look

  1. 1

    Make a character sheet first: front, side and back views of one design, always an adult.

  2. 2

    Write a fixed description: hair, eyes, ears or horns, build.

  3. 3

    For each image, add a short paragraph for the outfit and scene, and give a reference image with the order to keep its exact design but ignore its pose and background.

  4. 4

    Choose one tool and one recipe, because mixing sources breaks the look.

  5. 5

    Keep the prompt of every image you like, because old prompts exist nowhere else.

A custom add-on model, called a LoRALoRAA small add-on model trained so that an image generator reproduces one character or style. It keeps a persona’s look steady across images. It needs a set of consistent images and some technical work, and it is within reach of many people. A LoRA trained on a real person’s face without consent, or on a studio’s character sold as free to use, crosses a line of this wiki., keeps a character identical in every pose, but it takes a long phase of bugs and tuning. Generated art also ages as models change, so many doms keep old-style and new-style images in separate folders. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Two habits keep you out of trouble. Private tests stay private. A tool that copies the pose of an existing illustration, or that fetches reference images by tag and copies their prompts, can slide into art theft once its output is posted. Nothing derived from a named artist’s work should go public. Do not put a living artist’s name in a prompt either, because it borrows their style, so build the look on your own recipe. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Video costs more than stills. A failed attempt costs the same as a good one, and clips are made in short segments and joined, so many personas post a video only now and then. A persona also runs at a loss before it earns, so budget the tools against income you have and never finance them from a sub’s sends in a way that pressures him. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Strip hidden traces from every export, as Stay anonymous explains.

Who owns what

When another person makes the art, that person may treat it as theirs and expect credit, while you treat it as the persona’s body. Agree who owns and who is credited before the pictures spread. Borrowing another persona’s art is fine with permission, and permission is what separates it from theft. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Subs sometimes make fan art or generated images of their dom as a tribute, and some doms assign image making to a sub as a task. A task is a fair gift of skill only when the sub chose the scope, can say no without losing his place, and is thanked. Otherwise it is free labour dressed as devotion. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Throne says its wishlists cannot be used to exchange goods or services, including commissions.
