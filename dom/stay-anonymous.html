Becoming a dom Chapter 3

Stay anonymous

Last checked 3 November 20269 minutes to read

A drawn face cannot be leaked, but files, names, accounts, tools and payments can. This chapter shows where the person behind a persona is exposed, where anonymity ends, and why access to your accounts is never part of the game.

VerifiedChecked against the original source.ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A drawn persona is anonymous toward the audience. It is not anonymous toward the payout.
  • Only the persona’s name belongs in anything you produce. Clean every file, then check it.
  • Small public fields, game names and wishlist names connect accounts.
  • A real dom never needs a password, a login code or a remote session.
  • If it leaks, stop, secure, keep proof and ask for help.

You will learn

  • Who can learn who you are, and by which route.
  • How to clean files and sites, and how to check a tool before you run it.
  • Which public fields and names give a person away, for doms and for subs.
  • Why anonymity ends where the money lands.
  • What to do when a login request, a threat or a leak arrives.

What you hide, from whom

Anonymity in 2DFD is real and narrow. It works well toward the audience: a drawn face cannot be leaked, a clean file carries no coordinates, and a pseudonymous account shows no name. Compared with classic findom, the drawn character removes the face and the voice from the deal, and the risk moves to payment platforms, social platforms and the tools used to move money. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Some doms report being targeted by people who threaten them with fake explicit pictures made to look like them. Such threats frighten even when the pictures are known fakes. A drawn character has no real face to fake, which is a real layer of protection. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Secrecy has a cost. In a study of findommes, participants named secrecy among the most negative aspects of the work. Files, sites and tools

Only the persona’s name appears in anything you produce: files, file names, commits, metadata and screenshots. Never a personal name, a mail address or a system path that contains a user name. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Images carry traces: positions, colour profiles, comments, provenance records, prompts stored in text chunks and extra bytes. Videos carry a date, the name of the program and the name of the encoder. Clean every export, then reread the file and refuse the export if anything remains. Cleaning is needed even when you start clean, because an encoder can add a profile by itself. Test the checker with odd cases, because a hidden note placed in an unexpected spot once slipped through mine. Treat every picture as copyable too, since anyone can take a screenshot. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Publish an image

  1. 1

    Export the file from your tool.

  2. 2

    Strip it: location, profiles, comments, prompts and extra bytes.

  3. 3

    Reread the stripped file and compare it with a clean sample.

  4. 4

    If anything remains, stop and fix the cause, not the file.

  5. 5

    Only then post it.

A site leaks through its own plumbing. Serve everything from the site’s own host, with no third-party fonts, trackers or scripts, so that only the host sees a visit. Audit it from the outside: old versions of a site can stay reachable at hosting addresses you forgot. New adult-themed domains can be flagged early by browser safety filters, so test the name and keep a backup. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Code leaks too. Author commits under the persona’s name and in UTC, because a local time offset shows where the author lives. Create a code-hosting account with a pseudonym for both the name and the e-mail, because every change stays in the history. A tool you hand to others carries no name, no signature and no default picture. Strip the source, comments and leftover handles first, or someone can repackage it with something added. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Secrets never travel through a chat. Type a token or key into a masked prompt in your own terminal, never into a commit, screenshot or message, and send access keys privately so that they can be revoked. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A tool can be harmless in code and still unsafe in practice. Check what it sends and to whom, check its defaults and not only its screen, and test it in a virtual machine with no personal network. Do not run an unknown program on your main computer. Avoid any tool that builds an identifier from the computer name or the account name. A safer design starts with everything off, a whitelist, a random identifier, a short history and a control channel by invitation. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Anonymous confession features are anonymous only toward other members. The platform and the host still hold the data. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Accounts, names and public fields

Public fields leak more than files. A single careless bio line on a related account can show that the person behind a persona is not who the persona says. A game or stream name that resembles the persona’s name, or points back to an old dispute, can do the same. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Read every public field of every linked account as a stranger would.

Operators often run several accounts. Platform terms may forbid multiple accounts or a new account opened after a ban, and a second account puts the first at risk. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. Do not borrow another person’s phone number for a check. It drags an uninvolved person into adult activity. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Do not confirm or explain links between personas. When asked who a persona’s contacts or secondary identities are, deflect. Never link a handle you found in a tool’s files to a persona in public unless the person said so in their own words, because that is doxxingdoxTo expose a real identity or address. It is the main safety fear behind anonymity in the scene, so a request for a real name or for a gift that needs a physical address is a risk. It can strike the person behind a persona and the sub alike.. Exposing a bad actor also invites retaliation, such as mass reports from accounts the rival controls. If you show a mentor or a peer anything, show the persona account and never the personal one. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Purging messages and blocking contacts separates the persona from the person, and it also destroys learning material. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Deleting chats after every send is a different matter, because it can be a sign of the spiralspiralTwo senses. First, a rotating pattern that is the typical hypnotic image of the scene. Second, the spending spiral, in which spending that began as play tightens turn by turn from curious to chasing. Regret that keeps spending is an early warning, and every turn of the spending spiral has a way out, which Where to get help helps you find.. See The spiral.

If you are a sub

Names of senders can be visible on a public wishlist, and doms look at each other’s pages. Use a handle you are comfortable having seen by several doms. Keep a separate account for adult servers, and check who else is in a server before you join, because a mutual server can expose you. Lock the screen or close the chat on a shared computer, and use a separate profile. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Your name, your address, your workplace and your family are a legitimate boundary. A dom who needs them is not playing a game. Physical gifts break anonymity in both directions, so the safe default is no addresses, or a neutral forwarding or retail service. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Money is the weak point

Anonymity ends at the payout. In my experience a card processor or an adult payment company ties the persona to a legal identity: identity documents, a business registration and a bank account in the company’s country. An account opened in a real name carries any ban with it, and the ban follows the real person. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The persona is a mask toward the audience and the platforms, never toward the money rail. Partial or false details at a processor do not give anonymity. They lead to closed accounts and held funds, and a sub’s payment can vanish with them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The authors of a study of findommes flag fraud, including identity fraud, as a remaining risk in this field. A persona’s tools hold no names, mail addresses or cards. A card stays with the processor. Test any payment flow with a fake provider first, then in the provider’s test mode with its published test cards, and type a real key only in your own terminal. VVivian’s field notes

I track how these requests are phrased. The language of trust and the language of a scam overlap completely. When someone says a password proves you are serious, the request itself is the red flag, whatever the intention. A dom who means well does not need your password, and one who does not will say the same words.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Red flags

Access requests

  • A dom asks for your password as proof of honesty.
  • A dom asks for the one-time code just sent to your phone or e-mail.
  • A dom asks for a remote desktop session “to supervise”, and adds that you can always quit.
  • A dom logs you out of your own accounts, or keeps them open “for you”.
  • A dom asks for access as the price of forgiveness.

Counter. Say no once, calmly. Offer a screenshot or an export instead. If you already shared anything, change the password and sign out of every session. Do not argue.

The subs cannot speak freely, and the helper can step into conversations. If you share access, tell the subs, exclude named conversations, and remove the helper’s sessions when trust drops. When it leaks

Rumours and screenshots travel fast. A rumour that a dom is secretly a sub can cost her subs, and private images can spread through a shared server before an apology arrives. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Keep private images and chats out of any shared space.

If your identity leaks

  1. 1

    Stop. Do not confirm or deny a real-life detail.

  2. 2

    Secure your accounts: change passwords and sign out of every session.

  3. 3

    Save proof with dates before you block anyone.

  4. 4

    Report the account to the platform. Do not pay a blackmailer and do not send more material.

  5. 5

    Tell one person you trust, and read Where to get help.
