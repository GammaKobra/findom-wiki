Red flags

Payment requests a sub should refuse

  • A card number, a security code or a bank login typed into a chat.
  • A one-time code sent “to confirm” a payment.
  • A remote session offered “to help you pay”.
  • A payment link sent by message, not found on the persona’s own page.
  • A sudden new account or method, because “the old one was blocked”.

Counter. Pay only through the rail named on the persona’s own page, and check that page yourself. If a rail is down, wait. Nobody loses a game by waiting, and a real dom never needs your card details, your codes or your screen. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Choosing a rail and keeping a way out

Commission-only platforms are the realistic start for a small creator. Dedicated adult payment companies exist. They ask for identity documents, a registered business form, a matching bank account and fixed yearly costs, and their own pages give current terms. At small volumes the fixed costs alone can exceed the revenue. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Choose a rail

  1. 1

    Write down what you receive, per month or per year.

  2. 2

    Read the provider’s own list of prohibited businesses and its terms. Keep a dated copy.

  3. 3

    Write to the provider yourself. Ask in plain words whether a character-led adult persona is accepted, and ask about category, fees, any reserve, tax handling, payout currency and the use of a pseudonym on the public side.

  4. 4

    Do the sum. A commission-only platform charges more per payment and nothing fixed. With invented figures, a fixed cost of USD 1,500.00 a year and a commission 10 points lower break even against the platform at USD 15,000.00 of yearly sales.

  5. 5

    Decide only after a written answer. A vague answer counts as a no.

Sharing money, partners and wrong sends

Passing a wrong send on costs only fees, and a dom who forwards it at once and says so earns trust. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A tribute arrives for someone else

A sub writes that he meant to send to another persona and used your page by mistake. The gift is yours on paper.

When two doms share a sub’s money, or a dom pays a partner who builds tools, write the split in numbers before any work starts. A sub is not a flow of money to move between doms, and his consent is easy to lose. If he builds for you without pay, the scope is his to choose, he can refuse without losing his place, and he is thanked. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A percentage of the gross costs more than it looks. Suppose fees and tax leave you with 60 percent of each payment. A 15 percent cut of the gross is then 25 percent of what you keep. Fixed monthly pay creates pressure to out-earn it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Fees, tax and costs

Costs come before income: image and video generation, tools, hosting and the time to make them. Budget tools against income you already have, so that no single sub’s sends are needed to pay for them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Big figures traded in the scene are hearsay, and this wiki prints none as typical. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

In general terms, money a persona receives for her work is likely to count as income where you live, and the platform’s payout is not the end of the sum. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. Fees come off first, then tax, and sometimes sales tax or VAT on digital sales. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. Rules differ by country. Ask your tax authority or a tax adviser. This chapter gives no advice.
