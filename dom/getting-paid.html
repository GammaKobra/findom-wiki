Red flags

Payment requests a sub should refuse

  • A card number, a security code or a bank login typed into a chat.
  • A one-time code sent “to confirm” a payment.
  • A remote session offered “to help you pay”.
  • A payment link sent by message that isn’t on the persona’s own page.
  • A sudden new account or method, because “the old one was blocked”.

Counter. Pay only through the route named on the persona’s own page, and check that page yourself. If it’s down, wait. Nobody loses a game by waiting, and a real dom never needs your card details, your codes or your screen. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Choosing a payment route and keeping a way out

Commission-only platforms are the realistic start for a small creator. Dedicated adult payment companies exist. They ask for ID documents, a registered business form, a matching bank account and fixed yearly costs, and their own pages give the current terms. At small volumes the fixed costs alone can be more than you earn. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Choose a payment route

  1. 1

    Write down what you receive, per month or per year.

  2. 2

    Read the provider’s own list of prohibited businesses and its terms. Keep a dated copy.

  3. 3

    Write to the provider yourself. Ask in plain words whether a character-led adult persona is accepted, and ask about category, fees, any reserve, tax handling, payout currency and using a pseudonym on the public side.

  4. 4

    Do the sum. A commission-only platform takes more per payment and nothing fixed. With made-up figures, a fixed cost of USD 1,500.00 a year and a commission 10 points lower break even against the platform at USD 15,000.00 of yearly sales.

  5. 5

    Decide only after a written answer. A vague answer counts as a no.

Sharing money, partners and wrong sends

Passing a wrong send on only costs fees, and a dom who forwards it right away and says so earns trust. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A tribute arrives for someone else

A sub writes that he meant to send to another persona and used your page by mistake. On paper the gift is yours.

When two doms share a sub’s money, or a dom pays a partner who builds tools, write the split in numbers before any work starts. A sub isn’t a flow of money to pass between doms, and his consent is easy to lose. If he builds for you without pay, the scope is his to choose, he can say no without losing his place, and he gets thanked. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A percentage of the gross costs more than it looks. Say fees and tax leave you 60 percent of each payment. A 15 percent cut of the gross is then 25 percent of what you keep. Fixed monthly pay puts pressure on you to earn more than it costs. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Fees, tax and costs

Costs come before income: image and video generation, tools, hosting and the time it takes to make them. Budget tools against income you already have, so no single sub’s sends are needed to pay for them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The big figures people trade in the scene are hearsay, and this wiki prints none as typical. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

In general, money a persona gets for her work is likely to count as income where you live, and the platform’s payout isn’t the end of the sum. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. Fees come off first, then tax, and sometimes sales tax or VAT on digital sales. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. Rules differ by country. Ask your tax authority or a tax adviser.
