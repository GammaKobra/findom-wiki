Step 1 of 6 Before the first message: your frame

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Put your rules and limits in writing, in front of him before the first send.
  • The sub types his own limit while he’s calm. Nobody raises it in the middle of a session, and he can raise it only after a wait.
  • Ask what he wants, accept the answer, and accept “not today”.
  • Be harsh inside the scene and kind outside it. Never chase.
  • When a sub is in debt or in distress, the money part stops and outside help starts.

You will learn

  • What to show a sub before any money moves.
  • How to start small, with a limit he sets and a stop that works at once.
  • How to hold a consent conversation, and which answers to accept.
  • Which control habits to recognise and refuse, even when a mentor uses them.
  • How to let a sub leave well.

I treat a first sub as a person with real money before anything else. Keep three things and most other mistakes stay small: he sees the rules before the money, he types his own limit, and he can leave whenever he wants.

A dom who lets a sub skip the limit, or invites him past it because he keeps insisting, has moved from play to harm. When he insists, tighten the limit. Never lift it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Say in advance when a sub must not send and what happens if he does. In my experience a mild “please stop” said once doesn’t hold. A rule stated early, with a consequence you’ll really carry out, does. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The consent conversation

When a sub says “you decide”, tell him you asked because you want his answer. Ask which register he wants today, gentle or harsh, and treat it as a preference that can change from day to day. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Show pictures tied to different kinks and watch how he reacts. When he says one isn’t his, say that’s fine and move on. Drop a label he rejects, and drop a consequence that makes him uncomfortable. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Even a task announced without a choice can be countered. In the scene, negotiating before accepting is a normal part of consent. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Make it safe to ask. If a sub apologises for a request, tell him never to be sorry for asking, and if he confesses an urge, don’t call it a bad decision. Shame about what he wants makes a sub hide it and lie about it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Accept “not today”. A sub who feels low and asks to stop for the evening is released at once, and he owes no apology. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Which answer keeps the consent real?

  1. Question 1 of 2A sub writes: “I’d rather skip that one.” What do you answer?

    Answer. “Fine. Tell me what you’d like instead.”. A stated “not mine” is final. Telling him he’ll enjoy it turns his no into a problem to solve.

  2. Question 2 of 2Which sentence gives permission that’s really free?

    Answer. “Yes, I want to try it, and I can say stop.”. Permission that comes with a statement of hurt has a hidden price.

Running a session

Be harsh inside the scene and kind outside it. The persona’s cruelty is a tool. The person behind it treats the sub well, and after a hard scene a short check-in is aftercareaftercareCare after a scene to check that the other person is okay and calm. It’s a short, kind check-in, and a session that ends on a send with nothing after it has skipped it. Tools and apps rarely have anything like it, so the person running the game has to provide it.. A dom who’s gentle by nature can play harsh as a performance or stay gentle by default. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Going harder than the limits he named and the spending limit he set is how abuse works, so stay inside both. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Say exclusivity out loud. If you don’t want a sub to talk to or serve other doms, say so and get his agreement. Two doms can’t share a sub by deciding it between themselves. He has to agree, and one leads while the other stays at the edge. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What a sub tells you in confidence isn’t teaching material. Show a conversation only with his consent or with identifying details removed. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A dom with limited time can carry only a handful of active chats, and a newer dom fewer. New subs arrive slowly, and chatting for free with people who never pay wears you out. If you’re absent, you lose subs. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When a sub struggles or leaves

When a sub is struggling

When a sub says he’s struggling, pause the money part. Ask what’s wrong, listen, ask for nothing, and answer kindly. A sales offer at that moment is the wrong reply. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

He pays you, so you can’t be his safety net. Don’t manage his debts, cut down his spending yourself, or move him to a dom you think is better. That’s a conflict of interest. Ask a mentor early instead of carrying it alone. If he talks about having no future, he needs outside help: emergency services, a helpline, or a person physically near him. See Where to get help and When a sub is in trouble. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A sub who wants to quit may block the doms he knows, and that’s a legitimate way to avoid a relapse. Respect it and don’t write to him. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Your sub says he’s out of money and in debt

When a sub leaves

Every dom loses subs, some after a day and some after more than a year. The decent thing is to wish him well and leave it there: don’t chase him and don’t message him. If you hear a firm no, stop. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Fix a badly handled moment quickly. Don’t leave a sub alone in the middle of a conversation. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Ending well.

A pattern is a warning sign. It doesn’t settle what a person is. If you see several together, slow down and ask someone outside the relationship.
