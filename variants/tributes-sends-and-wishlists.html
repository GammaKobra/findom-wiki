Tributes, sends and wishlists

Last checked 3 November 20268 minutes to read

A tribute is a gift, a send is one click, and a wishlist is the menu behind both. This chapter explains what each one is, what the wishlist platform says about it, and where the pressure hides.

In 30 seconds

  • A tribute is a gift. It buys no service and no promise, and nobody can enforce one.
  • A wishlist works as a price menu. The names and prices on it shape what you send.
  • Fees, names and messages follow the platform’s rules, not the dom’s.

  • Set the number before the evening, hold it outside the moment, and keep a stop that always works.

The wishlist ladder and what it costs

A wishlist is a price menu. Items carry themed names and sit on a ladder from a token amount to a very large one. In my experience a ladder runs from about USD 1.00 to about USD 1,000.00, with one gift and one link per rung. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

An evening of sends, and the pressure inside it

A send evening has a shape, and knowing it is the best protection. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See also How an evening goes.

What you will see on your side

  1. 1

    The dom opens with a teaser or a short command.

  2. 2

    She posts one item link at a time, sometimes with an image.

  3. 3

    You click, pay and confirm.

  4. 4

    She answers with attention: praise, a pet name, an image.

  5. 5

    The next link arrives.

The pace is set by the dom, and the reward often arrives late or in part. That small delay keeps you looking toward the next send. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The same rhythm is built into Techdom and Games and casino play.

Set a cap and keep it

The capcapA limit on spending, set by the person who pays, in a calm moment, before the session. It is held outside the moment, in a server that enforces it, in a bank limit or with a trusted person, and it is raised only after a cooldown. Setting a cap that holds

  1. 1

    Choose the number while you are calm, from money you could lose without missing it. Never from borrowed money, rent or savings.

  2. 2

    Add the fees, so the number is the total that leaves your account.

  3. 3

    Keep the cap outside the moment: a bank limit, a separate account, or a person you trust.

  4. 4

    Raise it only after a waitcooldownA fixed wait that must pass before a decision to spend, or to raise a cap, takes effect. It gives a calm moment the chance to overrule an excited one, and a day is a sound length for any decision pushed by a deadline. A tool can enforce a cooldown as a minimum spacing between payments. of a night or more, never during an evening.

  5. 5

    Know your stop before you start: close the chat, mute the dom, leave the page. It must cost nothing and work at once.

If you run a wishlist, you hold the other half. Name your rungs truthfully, keep no public tally of sends, never name a sub’s balance, and stop the money part when a sub says it is short. Safe design often means earning less, and I say so. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Tribute that is not cash

Tribute does not have to be money. A wallpaper, a written message, fan art, a generated image or an in-game gift all count as devotion in the scene. A dom can assign work instead of money, and a skill is often the safer way to belong. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
