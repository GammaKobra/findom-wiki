In 30 seconds

  • A house-wins game is a loop with a known rule, a picture at each win and a clean way to lose.
  • With play chips, published odds and a Stop that works, it’s theatre.
  • With real money, a hidden total, a streak or a penalty, it’s a payment device.
  • Judge the total it takes to reach the guarantee, never the price of one pull.
  • Set your limit before you press anything, and leave any game that hides what it costs.

The house wins, and says so

A house-wins game always works the same way: a rule everyone knows, a picture at each win, effects that keep getting bigger, and the house wins in the end. You leave with nothing except a best score. The kinks behind it are simple: not being able to resist a bet, and losing at a casino. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Each one is a game with a clear start and an exit. See The kinks around money.

A house edgehouse edgeThe share of each bet the house keeps over time. A game that states its edge lets you read the odds, and a game that hides odds or prices is a pressure design. is an advantage that’s built in and stated openly. A rigged game hides its odds or bends them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Reading an odds window

A fair house-wins game shows a window with its rules and its house share. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Read it before your first bet.

Before the first bet

  1. 1

    Find the rules and the house share. If there’s no window, assume the odds are hidden.

  2. 2

    Ask where the numbers come from. A number read from the code that runs the game can be checked. A number on a poster can’t.

  3. 3

    Look for a rule that sounds small, like ties going to the house, and ask what it costs.

  4. 4

    Count all the money that goes through your hands, not only what you meant to lose, and set your limit now.

The house share is the part of each bet the house keeps over time. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. At 5%, the house keeps about USD 5.00 of every USD 100.00 you bet. Bet USD 100.00 twenty times and USD 2,000.00 has gone through your hands, and you can expect to lose about USD 100.00. A small percentage hides a big total.

Publishing the edge is honest. A near-miss is a separate trick: it makes a loss feel like you almost won, and a game can do both. A 10% edge is fine on play chips and a bad deal once real money moves. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Wheels and the prize table

A wheel spin lands on a prize or a price. In many tools a purchase prompt then sends the sub to the persona’s wishlist page to pay it. A prototype wheel made by Vivian has seven slices with fixed chances of 25, 25, 20, 12, 9, 6 and 3%. They add up to 100, and a test rejects any table that doesn’t. The player sees the table, so he knows the rarest slice comes up about three times in a hundred spins. In its first demo everything was simulated, and saying a gift had been received was just a button. I don’t claim it’s safe. It pays through a wishlist, so the Throne rules further down apply to it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A visible table is the main thing that separates a wheel from a trap. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Compare it with the designs below.

Red flags

Red flags in a wheel or a pull tool

  • The price shows up only as a multiplier or a label, so you can’t know the real total before you pay. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • A low result gets a line saying it’s not enough, then a forced respin. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • The prize table is missing, or its chances don’t add up.
  • A rare result opens a special mode where only higher amounts come up. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • A line tells you that losing here is the same as the pulls you already pay for. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Counter. Ask for the total before you press. If you can’t say it in one sentence, don’t play. Pick your amount first. A forced respin is a pressure feature, never a rule of the game. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

People defend hidden totals because unknown amounts earn more. The cost lands on the sub who’s short of money and keeps going because he can’t see the total. A flat price you can see is the safe design, even if it earns less. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The price of a guarantee

Files

pull_cost.py

"""The price to judge is the price of the guarantee, not the price of one pull."""

GUARANTEE_PULLS = 80
LIMIT = 50.00

print("Flat price per pull, shown before the charge")
for price in (3.00, 5.00):
    total = price * GUARANTEE_PULLS
    inside_limit = int(LIMIT // price)
    share = 100 * inside_limit / GUARANTEE_PULLS
    print(f"  USD {price:.2f} a pull: guarantee costs USD {total:.2f}; "
          f"a limit of USD {LIMIT:.2f} buys {inside_limit} pulls, {share:.0f} percent of the way")

print("Random price per pull, between USD 5.00 and USD 18.00")
low, high = 5.00, 18.00
print(f"  guarantee costs between USD {low * GUARANTEE_PULLS:.2f} and USD {high * GUARANTEE_PULLS:.2f}")
print(f"  a limit of USD {LIMIT:.2f} buys between {int(LIMIT // high)} and {int(LIMIT // low)} pulls")

print("A tool that never shows its total before it charges has no number to compare with a limit.")

Casino sessions and one-sided bets

Casino play can also be a live session instead of a tool. The sub plays blackjack, roulette or slots against the persona and sends what he bet. When he loses, he pays. When he wins, it costs the persona nothing real. The scene is theatre but the money is real, so it sits on the payment side of the line. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Only a limit set in advance, a total you can see and a Stop that works keep it a choice. This wiki describes the format and doesn’t endorse it. It’s a fantasy adults can choose. Real-money betting is something else, and the rules on it differ between countries and services. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

A hand is lost

In a casino session you lose a hand. The persona says one more would win it back. Your limit for the evening is set and half used.

A one-sided bet is built for you to lose. The dom dares the sub to resist something that’s nearly impossible to resist. There’s no prize for winning, and the house wins whatever happens. It’s a kink, and outside play it’s also a way to pressure someone for money. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Making a game safe to play

This part is for makers, and for anyone who wants to check one. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A checklist for a house-wins game

  1. 1

    Use play chips, or a flat price shown before each try and paid before it starts.

  2. 2

    Show the rules and the house share in a window, read from the code that runs the game, and measure the share by simulation before the game ships.

  3. 3

    Keep the controls visible and clickable under every picture and effect, with a Stop on every screen and Escape cutting everything in the same frame.

  4. 4

    Respect the reduced-motion setting, and keep flashes to three per second or fewer, measured and never judged by eye.

  5. 5

    Leave out streaks, daily penalties, rewards that fade, forced respins and guilt lines.

  6. 6

    Rehearse before going live, retest every link after any change, and never ship a switch that skips payment.

The flash limit is a published rule. WCAG says web pages must not flash more than three times in any one-second period, unless the flash is below the stated thresholds.
