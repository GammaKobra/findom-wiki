Tasks, customs and private sessions

Tasks, customs and sessions are the small building blocks of 2DFD play. Kept small, they’re cheap, easy to negotiate and easy to stop. When they’re used to steer you toward paying, that’s where the pressure hides.

Step 1 of 6 What a task is

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A task has a start, a rule, a proof and an end, and either person can stop it.
  • A task never replaces your limit. If it ends with a request to send, your limit still decides.
  • Service can stand in for money only when the sub chose what he does and can refuse.
  • A custom made from scratch is real work, so both sides should agree on a price they find fair.
  • Limits and a way to stop come before the first task, and a sub in trouble pauses the money.

You will learn

  • How a task, a ritual and a mission are built.
  • Which daily-life steps are cheap and which add up to a lot.
  • How to trade service for tribute on fair terms.
  • How a session runs from start to dismissal.
  • How to recognise a task or a session that is steering you towards a payment.

I give tasks because they let a sub spend his attention, his skill and his imagination instead of his money. I also know how the same setup gets used to soften someone up before asking for something. You’ll find both here, so you can tell them apart.

What a task is

A task has a clear start, an end, a rule and a proof. That makes it easy to follow and easy to stop. A deadline and a consequence are agreed beforehand, and a running count or a log in a dedicated room keeps score. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A typical small task: copy a phrase down a sheet of paper and send a photo of the sheet. Others are a set greeting instead of a plain hello, a picture as your wallpaper for a week with a screenshot as proof, or a pose confirmed by a check-in question before a reward comes. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Shape a task

  1. 1

    Name the start and the end, like seven days from tomorrow.

  2. 2

    Write the rule in one sentence.

  3. 3

    Pick a proof that shows the task and nothing else: a screenshot of a wallpaper or a sheet of paper, never a face, a name or personal data.

  4. 4

    Agree in advance what happens after a miss, and keep it small.

  5. 5

    Agree who can stop it, and when. The answer is both of you, at any time.

A dom can use the same setup on herself: a deadline, and a forfeit picked by whoever needs the push. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Rituals in daily life. Rituals keep a persona in your life between sends. A picture on your phone, a small gesture before you unlock it, a profile picture or banner switched to the dom’s branding, a dedicated account, merchandise. Each step is cheap. Together they make the dom hard to avoid. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Customs. A custom is a picture, a scene or a clip made on request. Customs can be earned as well as bought. A dom’s offer can ask for effort before payment, through tasks, slow training and customs you earn. Some customs are given free to keep the bond going. A session from start to dismissal

Sessions can be short. A micro session is about five to ten minutes of teasing and short commands, and it’s sometimes given free to keep the bond going. A text session uses a command, a role with typed actions, a task with proof, praise or scolding, and a dismissal. The main technique is call and response, with repeated statements and typed lines. A guessing game can keep a sub chatting at no cost. Session menus are often split into groups like drain sessions, techdom, roleplay and gamedom. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  1. 1

    Agree on the frame first: the theme, the limits, the stop word and the spending limit.

  2. 2

    Open with the short ritual, and keep it short.

  3. 3

    In the middle, use commands, a role with typed actions, or a task with a safe proof.

  4. 4

    If money is part of the game, it stays inside the limit the sub set beforehand. The dom asks for nothing beyond it, keeps the running total visible, and stops when he says so.

  5. 5

    Close with praise or a scolding you both agreed to, then dismiss him.

  6. 6

    Finish with a short check that he’s okay. See aftercareaftercareCare after a scene to check that the other person is okay and calm. It’s a short, kind check-in, and a session that ends on a send with nothing after it has skipped it. Tools and apps rarely have anything like it, so the person running the game has to provide it..

Negotiating is normal. Even when a dom announces a task with no choice, the sub can propose something else, and the dom can counter. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Gating, denial and the pressure to pay

Gating means holding back a reward until the sub begs or does a small act. It builds anticipation and keeps the dom in control of the pace. Denial adds a rule that the sub can’t finish, sometimes for a set time. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Both are play between consenting adults when they’re negotiated and either person can stop. Long periods of denial, and wearing a chastity device for a long time, can carry physical risks. Limits, stopping and the dom’s side

Limits come before the first task. In my experience a new dom often doesn’t know how to find a sub’s limits, and a sub often doesn’t know how to say them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Find a limit and make a way out

  1. 1

    Ask before the first task: what’s a yes, what’s a maybe, and what’s a hard no. See hard limithard limitA firm boundary that someone will not cross. A dom can set one on how much contact she will take on, and a sub can set one on words, topics or money..

  2. 2

    Agree on a safety word, or an ordinary phrase, that ends everything at once. See safety wordsafety wordA word agreed when consent is given that anyone can use to stop the play at once. It stops play between people, and the other person stops, answers calmly and doesn’t argue..

  3. 3

    Start small, and ask afterwards how it felt.

  4. 4

    Check in regularly, because a limit can move either way.

  5. 5

    When he stops, stop. Don’t bargain, and don’t punish him for a no.

  6. 6

    If he says money is short or he’s not well, the money part ends for the evening. See When a sub is in trouble.

The person getting paid can’t be the safety net. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. A dom can stop, listen, ask for nothing more and point him elsewhere. A crisis needs outside help. See Where to get help. Self-harm, distress and crisis never belong in a task.

A dom needs limits too. She can refuse a request, refuse a price that ignores the work, and end a session. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
