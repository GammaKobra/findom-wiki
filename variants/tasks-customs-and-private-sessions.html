Variants Chapter 7

Tasks, customs and private sessions

Last checked 3 November 20269 minutes to read

Tasks, customs and sessions are the small structures of 2DFD play. Kept small, they are cheap, negotiable and easy to stop. Used as a sales funnel, they are where pressure hides.

ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A task has a start, a rule, a proof and an end, and either person can stop it.
  • A task never replaces the cap. If it ends with a request to send, the cap still decides.
  • Service can stand in for money only when the sub chose the scope and can refuse.
  • A custom made new is real work, so both sides should agree a price they find fair.
  • Limits and a stop come before the first task, and a sub in trouble pauses the money.

You will learn

  • How a task, a ritual and a mission are built.
  • Which daily-life steps are cheap and which add up to a lot.
  • How to trade service for tribute on fair terms.
  • How a session runs from start to dismissal.
  • How to recognise a task or a session that is steering you towards a payment.

A good task is small enough to finish and clear enough to stop. I give tasks because they let a sub spend his attention, his skill and his imagination without spending money. I also know how the same structure is used to soften someone up before a request. This chapter describes both, so that you can tell them apart.

What a task is

A task has a clear start, an end, a rule and a proof. A deadline and a consequence are agreed beforehand, and a running count or a log in a dedicated room keeps score. Clear start, end, rule and proof make a task easy to follow and easy to stop. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Typical small tasks are copying a phrase down a sheet and sending a photo of the sheet. Others are a fixed greeting ritual in place of a plain hello, a picture set as the wallpaper for a week with a screenshot as proof, or a pose confirmed by a check-in question before a reward arrives. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Shape a task

  1. 1

    Name the start and the end, such as seven days from tomorrow.

  2. 2

    Write the rule in one sentence.

  3. 3

    Choose a proof that shows the task and nothing else: a screenshot of a wallpaper or a sheet of paper, never a face, a name or personal data.

  4. 4

    Agree the consequence of a miss in advance, and keep it small.

  5. 5

    Agree who can stop it, and when. The answer is both of you, at any time.

A dom can use the same structure on herself: a deadline, and a forfeit chosen by the person who needs the push. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A task is not a way around the cap. Tasks can build anticipation, withhold a reward and create frustration, and a request to send that follows is easier to accept for it. Rituals and the daily-life footprint. Rituals keep a persona present between sends. A picture on the phone, a small gesture before unlocking, a profile picture or banner changed to the dom’s branding, a dedicated account, merchandise. Each step is cheap. Together they make the dom hard to avoid. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Service, customs and fair terms

A sub with technical skills can code, generate images, edit video, sort a large library of pictures or write captions, and a title and a channel for updates turn that into a role. Customs. A custom is a picture, a scene or a clip made on request. Customs can be earned and not only bought. An offer line can place effort before payment, with tasks, slow training and customs earned. Some customs are given free to keep a bond warm. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A custom from a drawn persona is nearly free, because she is only a drawing.

    Fact

    In my experience a custom is real work when it is made new, even for a drawn persona. A request that pays a small amount for each picture of a long roleplay is a loss for the person who makes them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Everything a persona makes must be paid for.

    Fact

    Some customs are given at no price, and some are earned through tasks. What matters is that the terms are clear before the work starts. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Some subs fear that asking for a custom will spoil a friendly chat. A separate channel for requests keeps the chat relaxed and gives commerce its own place. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A session from start to dismissal

A session is a small structure. A micro session lasts about five to ten minutes of teasing and short commands, and it is sometimes given free to keep the bond warm. A text session uses a command, a role with typed actions, a task with proof, praise or scolding, and a dismissal, and the main technique is call and response with repeated statements and typed lines. A guessing game can keep a sub chatting at no cost. A session menu is often grouped into families such as drain sessions, techdom, roleplay and gamedom. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  1. 1

    Agree the frame first: the theme, the limits, the stop word and the cap.

  2. 2

    Open with the short ritual, and keep the opening short.

  3. 3

    In the middle, use commands, a role with typed actions or a task with a safe proof.

  4. 4

    If money is part of the game, it stays inside the cap the sub set beforehand. The dom asks for nothing beyond it, keeps the running total visible, and stops when he says so.

  5. 5

    Close with praise or a scolding that both of you agreed to, and then dismiss.

  6. 6

    Finish with a short check that he is well. See aftercareaftercareCare after a scene to check that the other person is well and calm. It is a short, kind check-in, and a session that ends on a send with nothing after it has skipped it. Tools and apps rarely have an equivalent, so the person who runs the game has to supply it..

Negotiation is normal. Even when a dom announces a task with no choice, the sub can propose another arrangement, and the dom may counter. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Gating, denial and the pressure to pay

Gating is withholding a reward until the sub begs or performs a small act. It builds anticipation and keeps the dom in control of pacing. Denial adds a rule that the sub may not finish, sometimes for a set time. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Both are consenting-adult play when they are negotiated and either person can stop. Long periods of denial, and wearing a chastity device for a long time, can carry physical risks. Limits, stopping and the dom’s side

Limits come before the first task. In my experience a new dom often does not know how to find a sub’s limits, and a sub often does not know how to say them. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Find a limit and make a way out

  1. 1

    Ask before the first task: what is a yes, what is a maybe, and what is a hard no. See hard limithard limitA firm boundary that someone will not cross. A dom can set one on how much contact she will take on, and a sub can set one on words, topics or money..

  2. 2

    Agree a safety word, or an ordinary phrase, that ends everything at once. See safety wordsafety wordA word agreed when consent is given that anyone can use to stop the play at once. It stops play between people, and the other person stops, answers calmly and does not argue..

  3. 3

    Start small, and ask afterwards how it felt.

  4. 4

    Check in regularly, because a limit can move in either direction.

  5. 5

    When he stops, stop. Do not bargain, and do not punish him for a no.

  6. 6

    If he says money is short or he is not well, the money part ends for the evening. See When a sub is in trouble.

The person who is paid cannot be the safety net. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. A dom can stop, listen, ask for nothing more and point outward, and a crisis calls for outside help. See Where to get help. Self-harm, distress and crisis are not subjects for a task.

A dom needs limits too. A dom can refuse a request, refuse a price that ignores the work, and end a session. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
