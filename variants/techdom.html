Step 1 of 5 What techdom is

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Techdom means a tool does part of the dominating, with the sub’s agreement given beforehand and a stop he can use at any moment.
  • A tool can take three things from you: money, attention or control of your machine. The further down that list, the more you hand over.
  • Doms like it because it earns while they’re away. Subs like it because it takes the decision off them, and that’s also its main danger.
  • Remote control of your computer is the riskiest offer, and the request is the same one a common scam makes.
  • Trust a tool for the limits you can see, never for the dom’s word.

A tool can hold the rules better than a person can. The only one I’d trust has three things built in where nobody can change them mid-session: an amount, a limit and a way out.

What techdom is

techdomtechdomDominance through technology, like extensions, apps or tools, so the dom earns without putting in live effort each time. It’s a game between consenting adults, always with a limit, a wait and a stop the sub controls. Remote access is never part of the game. is the part of 2DFD where a tool does the dominating. The dom sets the rules, and software on the sub’s own computer, phone or accounts carries them out. The usual examples are an extension that sends gifts on its own, and one that likes and reposts the dom’s posts from the sub’s own account. There are also apps that set his wallpaper or lock his device, and live sessions where the dom drives his screen. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Some creators use the word more loosely, for any corner of the scene where someone builds something with tech instead of just talking. Here it means the narrow thing, because that’s the version that can be made safe: a tool acts for the dom, the sub agreed beforehand, and he has a stop that works at every moment. Handing a decision to someone else and to a machine is a fantasy adults are free to choose. Done well, the machine shows you the rules in numbers. Done badly, it takes away the pause where you’d change your mind. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Remote sessions

A remote session gets sold as a private session. The dom connects to the sub’s computer, sometimes for hours, and may change his wallpaper, watch him play, or act as him on his accounts. Usually she asks him to approve full control and block his own keyboard and mouse, so he can’t interrupt. She can see everything open on the machine: files, saved passwords, mail and chats. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

It’s the riskiest thing techdom offers, and it’s the same move a well-known scam uses. Public agencies tell people not to give a stranger control of their computer or phone. Red flags

A remote-access request is a red flag when

  • It comes from someone you met recently, or late in a long, warm chat.
  • It’s framed as a joke, a dare or a test of trust.
  • It asks you to read out a connection code, or to install software you didn’t look up.
  • It asks you to block your own keyboard and mouse.
  • It will run while you’re away from the screen.

Counter. Say no to the remote part and offer screenshots, or a shared screen you can end yourself. They cover almost every purpose, and nobody who plays fair needs more.

Saying no costs nothing. Here’s how to do it and keep the stop in your hands.

How to refuse a remote session

  1. 1

    Say no to remote control in plain words, however warm the chat has been.

  2. 2

    Offer screenshots instead, or a shared screen that you start, limit to one window and end yourself.

  3. 3

    Never install remote-access software because someone asks, and never read out a connection code.

  4. 4

    Never agree to block your own keyboard and mouse. Blocked input takes away your stop.

  5. 5

    If you already gave access, end the connection yourself, remove the program, change any password that was visible on screen, and read Where to get help.

What consent looks like in a tool

You agree to a tool before the session, in plain words, and you can take that agreement back. A yes given in the moment is weak: one press account asks whether it’s really consent when a client is too aroused to think straight about taking on debt. A tool can be a faster way to hand over the same thing.

Trust the design of a tool, never the dom’s word. Whoever runs a tool can use whatever it reaches, at any moment. A techdom tool you can trust shows its limits, has an emergency stop, keeps a record of what it can see, and can’t do anything without the sub. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. A tool that respects consent usually looks like this:

  • It arrives switched off, and it does one named thing on one named site.
  • You set the limit before you start, and something other than the page you’re looking at enforces it.
  • A running total is always visible, and a payment only counts once it’s confirmed.
  • A stop is always on screen, works in every state of the tool, and costs nothing.
  • Any control program has a quick exit, like a shortcut or a button, and a safety word agreed beforehand.
  • A command you didn’t accept inside the program never runs.
  • A trigger, like a sound you were taught to obey, has an agreed way to be switched off.

The design rules behind this list are in Safe design.

Myths about tools

  • Myth

    A techdom tool only affects my computer.

    Fact

    A tool that acts through your accounts or your card reaches the services tied to them. Your social account can be limited, your bank sees every payment, and a remote session reaches whatever’s open. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    If I trust the dom, I don’t need limits.

    Fact

    Trusting a person doesn’t set a limit. A limit, a wait and a stop work whether or not she deserves your trust. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Silent sends mean a happy sub.

    Fact

    A recurring send with no message may be a tool running with nobody watching. Check in before you enjoy it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I can always close the program, so I don’t need a stop.

    Fact

    Closing the window is the last resort. A tool needs a visible stop, a pause and a limit of its own. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
