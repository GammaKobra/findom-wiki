Variants Chapter 6

Roleplay, lore and collabs

Last checked 3 November 202610 minutes to read

A persona is a story told in public, and two personas can tell one together. The creative part is cheap and fun. The trouble starts when a sub or a payment link enters the story.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Lore is a frame, a tone and a few repeatable assets, not a long document.
  • Most collab forms are cheap and sound. Shared money and shared subs are where they go wrong.
  • Permission, credit and written terms separate a collab from theft.
  • A sub shared between two doms needs rules, and his part has to be optional.
  • A calendar or a rank that rewards streaks of sending turns play into obligation.

You will learn

  • How a persona’s lore and assets are built from small pieces.
  • Which collab forms exist, what they cost and where they break.
  • How to ask permission, share credit and end a collab cleanly.
  • How to share a sub without turning him into a prize.
  • How to read a hierarchy, a rank ladder or an event calendar from the sub’s side.

A persona is a story told in public, and a good story is cheap to share. Two characters on one screen cost two artists an afternoon. The trouble starts when a sub or a payment link enters the frame, because then the story has a bill. I like collabs, and I write the terms down before the first post.

Lore and persona assets

Lore is the frame around a character: who she is, what her world believes, how she speaks. A cult or a religion is a common frame, because posts and profile already mention members and doctrine. From a sub’s side, a cult frame can make leaving feel like betrayal. It is still a costume, and leaving costs nothing. A frame with no visible way out has become pressure; see Pressure tactics, both ways. The tone can mix maternal warmth, a princess image and affectionate humiliation. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Good lore needs a long document.

    Fact

    Names, wording, domain names and prompts carry the lore, so a frame, a tone and a handful of repeatable assets are enough to start. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A story has to stay inside one account.

    Fact

    A story can run as an ongoing scene that the audience answers in text, such as a character who walks into a hypnotic cafe. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Lore also gives roleplay a shape. A three-step ladder of titles can be awarded for activity and devotion, with the senior dom deciding who is promoted. Ranks like this keep members posting and invested, which is why they need care. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The hierarchy section below looks at them from the sub’s side.

A persona is built from pieces that can be made again and again. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Cut out a character cleanly

  1. 1

    Ask for the character on a flat green background.

  2. 2

    Remove the background by colour distance.

  3. 3

    Avoid shadows on the wall and tight crops that put hands or chest under the page text.

  4. 4

    Check the edges and any detail that returns despite the prompt, such as an accessory you did not ask for.

A small collab piece can be sized at about four hours of work. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The four-hour recipe

  1. 1

    Each persona writes a short sheet on her style and lore.

  2. 2

    Both list ideas of domination in the broad sense that fit both characters.

  3. 3

    Pick one simple original production built on the lore of one of them.

  4. 4

    Post it on both accounts, each in her own voice.

  5. 5

    Add a few small optional actions for the audience. A fictional auction is fine when no real money moves.

Permission, credit and money between partners

Permission and credit separate a collab from theft. Borrowing another persona’s art needs her permission, and an artist-persona is often flattered when asked. The same goes for turning a partner’s photo into an image of your own persona. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • A fan or sub who makes content about someone else’s persona agrees the use first: an outfit that is off limits, viewing only, no public posting. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • A partner’s private material and lore stay out of your files unless she agrees in writing. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • The owner of a character chooses which looks may be used. She may ask that her signature design stays out of a derivative and that alternate outfits are used. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

This is the same line that Art for your persona and Stolen art and persona theft draw from the other side. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Your partner’s tool could be copied

You have an idea that is a close copy of a tool another dom released. You are about to publish.

Paying a tool-making partner. One person builds, tests and edits media, and the other supplies images and text and does the posting. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The money between them needs a clear arrangement, and each arrangement has its own pressure. This wiki gives no tax advice, and each arrangement can have tax effects.

A person who helps as a second dom or a builder should get something back, though not necessarily money. Unpaid work breeds resentment and burnout. Any deal that moves a sub’s money to a partner without his knowledge is a conflict of interest. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When a collab ends

  1. 1

    Keep going with what was agreed.

  2. 2

    Leave out the part that was cancelled.

  3. 3

    Do not call the other out in public.

  4. 4

    Respect a no.

  5. 5

    Write down who keeps what.

Unclear terms are what end most collabs. Two accounts bidding against each other to inflate a price is deception, and the wiki does not treat it as a collab. A cross-promotion deal such as a retweet game falls apart in the same way when one side backs out and the terms were never clear. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Two doms, one sub

A sub shared between two doms becomes a resource between two people. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. If it happens, the sub has to be told, and his part has to be optional.

  • Myth

    Two doms cost a sub no more, because a sub with a fixed weekly amount sends the same total whichever dom prompts him.

    Fact

    A second dom adds pressure behind the scenes and can raise the total. The amount has to be the sub’s cap, held by him, and never the sum of two doms’ hopes. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Rules for sharing a sub

  1. 1

    Ask him first. Sharing is optional, and he can say no without losing his place.

  2. 2

    Tell him in plain words that it is a team, not a competition.

  3. 3

    Tell him plainly how any gift is split between you, and let him choose. Nothing about his sends is arranged between the doms unless he has agreed.

  4. 4

    Hold no double sessions at the same time.

  5. 5

    Tell the other dom before any decision that affects him.

  6. 6

    Keep nothing hidden, including a channel about his sends.

  7. 7

    His cap belongs to him, and the doms do not add their caps together.

  8. 8

    Raise jealousy calmly and in private.

If he says money is short, all sending stops for the evening on both sides. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Jealousy between doms over a shared sub is normal, and the calm way is to bring it up in private. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Hierarchies and chains of money

A betadommebetadommeA junior or secondary dom who works beside an established one, in some communities as a helper or trainee. The role is worth little to the junior unless there is a clear reason such as training or experience, and it needs agreed terms like any unpaid work. The word also names a kink in which a domme is made to lose to a stronger dom. is a junior dom who serves a more senior one, and in the kink sense she passes up part of what she collects from her own subs. A faildommefaildommeA persona that plays a failing dom who is beaten, drained or humiliated by a stronger one. It is a hashtag and a persona type, and also the loose label for a dom who falls short of the role. receives tributes from her own followers and hands them to a bigger dom, who humiliates her for it. Both are consenting-adult kink and a content genre. The senior’s status is shown by how many doms serve her, and tribute art of personas kneeling to her is a common sign. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The money flows up: from small subs through a lesser persona to a stronger dom. If a sub says money is short, the money part stops for the evening, wherever he sits in the chain. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Possessiveness is a common dynamic: the dom feigns jealousy when a sub talks to other doms, threatens in character and asks for an oath of loyalty. It works as roleplay, and it works as a retention device. The line is easy to state. The persona may be jealous inside the scene, and she never decides who the sub speaks to outside it. Red flags

A triangle where the senior gains from your loss

  • A senior sets two juniors against each other so that both depend on her more. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • The rivalry leaves both juniors weaker and more attached to her. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Counter. Rivalry games must be optional for the juniors, and a junior must be able to leave without a penalty. A game that only the senior can end is control.

Red flags

A request to pass money or content on

  • A stranger asks you to receive content or money and forward it to a third dom. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • The reason given is a kink that he cannot act on himself. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Counter. Decline. You would become the middleman and carry the risks. Describe the pattern if you warn others, and name nobody.

Seasonal events and streaks

A seasonal event gives a persona a month-long story. A persona may plan one post a day on the theme, with a program of text and image written in advance for each day, and a calendar on the site with one door per day. The seasonal look is a layer on top of the base style: switched on for the month, off afterwards, and lighter on slow machines. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

An event month

  1. 1

    Before the month

    Write the program for every day, and build the look as a layer on top of the base style. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    The first day

    The look is switched on and the first door opens.

  3. 3

    Every day

    One post on the theme, and one door on the calendar.

  4. 4

    The end of the month

    The layer is switched off and the story closes.

A calendar should be fair to the person who opens it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What a fair calendar does

  1. 1

    The server judges time

    The calendar reads the clock of the server and never the clock of the phone. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    A door never opens early

    Nobody gains a day by changing the phone’s date. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  3. 3

    A missed door says so

    The page tells the visitor plainly that the door is closed. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  4. 4

    A failed save is shown

    Progress that does not save is visible, so nobody loses a day silently. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A prize for finishing every door rewards loyalty.

    Fact

    A top prize given only to those who opened every door on its day and made every send pushes people to spend in order to avoid losing what they have already put in. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A rank ladder is only decoration.

    Fact

    Ranks and streaks borrow from games to create obligation, and a rank that moves with sending is a pressure design. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
