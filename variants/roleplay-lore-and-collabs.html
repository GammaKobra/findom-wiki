Roleplay, lore and collabs

A persona is a story told in public, and two personas can tell one together. The creative part is cheap and fun. Trouble starts when a sub or a payment link gets into the story.

Step 1 of 6 Lore and persona assets

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Lore is a frame, a tone and a few assets you can reuse. You don’t need a long document.
  • Most collab forms are cheap and work fine. Shared money and shared subs are where they go wrong.
  • Permission, credit and written terms are what separate a collab from theft.
  • A sub shared between two doms needs rules, and his part has to be optional.
  • A calendar or a rank that rewards streaks of sending turns play into obligation.

You will learn

  • How a persona’s lore and assets are built from small pieces.
  • Which collab forms exist, what they cost and where they break.
  • How to ask permission, share credit and end a collab cleanly.
  • How to share a sub without turning him into a prize.
  • How to read a hierarchy, a rank ladder or an event calendar from the sub’s side.

Two characters on one screen cost two artists an afternoon. Once a sub or a payment link gets involved, there’s money in it, and someone pays. I like collabs, and I write the terms down before the first post.

Lore and persona assets

Lore is the world around a character: who she is, what her world believes, how she talks. A cult or a religion is a common choice, because posts and profiles already talk about members and doctrine. If you’re the sub, a cult setting can make leaving feel like betrayal. It’s still a costume, and leaving costs you nothing. When there’s no visible way out, it’s turned into pressure. See Pressure tactics, both ways. The tone can mix maternal warmth, a princess image and affectionate humiliation. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Good lore needs a long document.

    Fact

    Names, wording, domain names and prompts carry the lore. A frame, a tone and a handful of assets you can reuse are enough to start. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A story has to stay inside one account.

    Fact

    A story can run as an ongoing scene that the audience answers in text, like a character who walks into a hypnotic cafe. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Lore also gives roleplay a structure. A ladder of three titles can be handed out for activity and devotion, with the senior dom deciding who moves up. Ranks like this keep members posting and invested, which is why they need care. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The hierarchy section below looks at them from the sub’s side.

A persona is built from pieces you can make again and again. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Cut out a character cleanly

  1. 1

    Ask for the character on a flat green background.

  2. 2

    Remove the background by colour distance.

  3. 3

    Avoid shadows on the wall, and tight crops that put hands or chest under the page text.

  4. 4

    Check the edges, and any detail that comes back despite the prompt, like an accessory you didn’t ask for.

A small collab piece takes about four hours of work. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The four-hour recipe

  1. 1

    Each persona writes a short sheet on her style and lore.

  2. 2

    Both list ideas of domination, in the broad sense, that fit both characters.

  3. 3

    Pick one simple, original piece built on the lore of one of them.

  4. 4

    Post it on both accounts, each in her own voice.

  5. 5

    Add a few small optional actions for the audience. A fictional auction is fine when no real money moves.

Permission, credit and money between partners

Permission and credit are what separate a collab from theft. To borrow another persona’s art you need her permission, and an artist-persona is often flattered to be asked. The same goes for turning a partner’s photo into an image of your own persona. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • A fan or sub who makes content about someone else’s persona agrees with her first on how it can be used, such as an outfit that’s off limits, viewing only, or nothing posted publicly. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • A partner’s private material and lore stay out of your files unless she agrees in writing. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • The owner of a character chooses which looks can be used. She can ask that her signature design stays out of a derivative, and that alternate outfits are used. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Art for your persona and Stolen art and persona theft draw the same line from the other side. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Your partner’s tool could be copied

You have an idea that’s a close copy of a tool another dom released. You’re about to publish.

Paying a tool-making partner. One person builds, tests and edits media. The other supplies images and text and does the posting. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The money between them needs a clear deal, and each kind of deal brings its own pressure. Each one can also have tax effects, and this wiki gives no tax advice.

Someone who helps as a second dom or a builder should get something back, even if it isn’t money. Unpaid work leads to resentment and burnout. Any deal that moves a sub’s money to a partner without him knowing is a conflict of interest. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When a collab ends

  1. 1

    Keep doing what was agreed.

  2. 2

    Drop the part that was cancelled.

  3. 3

    Don’t call the other person out in public.

  4. 4

    Respect a no.

  5. 5

    Write down who keeps what.

Most collabs end over unclear terms. Two accounts bidding against each other to push a price up is deception, and this wiki doesn’t count it as a collab. A cross-promotion deal like a retweet game falls apart the same way when one side backs out and the terms were never clear. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Two doms, one sub

A sub shared between two doms becomes a resource two people draw on. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. If it happens, he has to be told, and his part has to be optional.

  • Myth

    Two doms cost a sub no more, because a sub with a fixed weekly amount sends the same total whichever dom asks.

    Fact

    A second dom adds pressure behind the scenes and can push the total up. The amount has to be the sub’s own limit, kept by him, and never the sum of what two doms hope for. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Rules for sharing a sub

  1. 1

    Ask him first. Sharing is optional, and he can say no without losing his place.

  2. 2

    Tell him in plain words that you’re working as a team and nobody is competing for him.

  3. 3

    Explain how any gift is split between you, and let him choose. Nothing about his sends is arranged between the doms unless he’s agreed.

  4. 4

    Don’t hold double sessions at the same time.

  5. 5

    Tell the other dom before any decision that affects him.

  6. 6

    Keep nothing hidden, not even a channel about his sends.

  7. 7

    His limit belongs to him, and the doms don’t add their limits together.

  8. 8

    Bring up jealousy calmly and in private.

If he says money is short, all sending stops for the evening on both sides. Jealousy between doms over a shared sub is normal. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Hierarchies and chains of money

A betadommebetadommeA junior or second dom who works beside an established one, in some communities as a helper or trainee. The role isn’t worth much to the junior unless there’s a clear reason like training or experience, and it needs agreed terms like any unpaid work. The word also names a kink where a domme is made to lose to a stronger dom. is a junior dom who serves a senior one. In the kink sense, she passes up part of what she collects from her own subs. A faildommefaildommeA persona that plays a failing dom who gets beaten, drained or humiliated by a stronger one. It’s a hashtag and a persona type, and also a loose label for a dom who falls short of the role. takes tributes from her own followers and hands them over to a bigger dom, who humiliates her for it. Both are kinks between consenting adults, and a content genre too. A senior’s status shows in how many doms serve her. Tribute art of personas kneeling to her is a common sign. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The money flows up, from small subs through a lesser persona to a stronger dom. If a sub says money is short, the money part stops for the evening, wherever he is in the chain. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Possessiveness is common: the dom acts jealous when a sub talks to other doms, makes threats in character and asks him to swear loyalty. It works as roleplay, and it also works to keep him from leaving. The line is simple. The persona can be jealous inside the scene, and she never decides who the sub talks to outside it. Red flags

A triangle where the senior gains from your loss

  • A senior sets two juniors against each other so that both depend on her more. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • The rivalry leaves both juniors weaker, and more attached to her. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Counter. Rivalry games have to be optional for the juniors, and a junior has to be able to leave without a penalty. A game only the senior can end is control.

Red flags

A request to pass money or content on

  • A stranger asks you to receive content or money and pass it on to a third dom. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • The reason he gives is a kink he can’t act on himself. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Counter. Say no. You’d become the middleman and carry the risks. If you warn others, describe the pattern and name nobody.

Seasonal events and streaks

A seasonal event gives a persona a story that lasts a month. She can plan one post a day on the theme, with the text and image for each day written in advance, and a calendar on her site with one door per day. The seasonal look is a layer on top of the base style: on for the month, off afterwards, and lighter on slow machines. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

An event month

  1. 1

    Before the month

    Write each day’s program, and build the look as a layer on top of the base style. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    The first day

    The look goes on and the first door opens.

  3. 3

    Every day

    One post on the theme, and one door on the calendar.

  4. 4

    The end of the month

    The layer goes off and the story ends.

A calendar should be fair to the person who opens it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What a fair calendar does

  1. 1

    The server keeps the time

    The calendar reads the server’s clock, never the phone’s. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    A door never opens early

    Nobody gets a day ahead by changing the date on their phone. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  3. 3

    A missed door says so

    The page tells the visitor plainly that the door is closed. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  4. 4

    A failed save is shown

    If progress doesn’t save, the page shows it, so nobody loses a day without knowing. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A prize for finishing every door rewards loyalty.

    Fact

    A top prize given only to people who opened every door on its day and made every send pushes them to spend so they don’t lose what they’ve already put in. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A rank ladder is only decoration.

    Fact

    Ranks and streaks borrow from games to create obligation. A rank that moves with sending is a pressure design. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A calendar can reward showing up, a comment or a drawing, and leave money out of it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Pressure tactics, both ways. If a streak or a chain is costing you more than you planned, see The spiral and Where to get help.
