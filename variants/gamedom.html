This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Gamedom is domination that uses a game as its stage: playing together, tasks, staged scenes, and sometimes the loss of a real account.
  • Most of it is play. The part that takes something from you for good is the real destruction of an account you built.
  • Handing over a game login hands over more than a game, and many online games forbid sharing an account, so check the rules.
  • What’s sold is the feeling. A staged scene gives you most of it, and you keep the account.
  • Gacha spending is real money. Your limit applies here like everywhere else.

Gacha cuck explained

Gacha cuck is a kink where a dom visibly destroys or takes a sub’s game account, throwing away his spending or his characters. What makes it work is how attached he is: the more he built, the stronger the scene. It’s often shown through edits, and viewers have noticed that some of them look staged. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

As a performance, that’s fine. Passing it off as real to other subs is lying to them. In my experience a dom rarely destroys items for real, because what sells is the feeling. Account fixing: what changes hands

In account fixing, the sub hands over a game login, and the dom plays while he watches, or without him seeing anything until she’s done. Close cousins are game tasks for any title, and live tasks where the sub shares his screen and follows orders. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Only the live task leaves your login with you. A game account is often tied to a saved payment method, a mail address and other services, and whoever holds the login can reach all of it. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. This wiki hasn’t checked every publisher’s terms, but online games commonly forbid sharing an account, so read your game’s rules before you hand anything over. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

It’s also a scam script. A stranger who wants a login, a recovery address or a one-time code isn’t asking to play. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Red flags

A request for your game login is a red flag when

  • It comes too early, too fast, or from someone you can’t check.
  • It asks for the recovery mail, a login code or the second step of your sign-in.
  • It targets your main account, the one with the most spending behind it.
  • It asks you to buy something in the game first, as proof.

Counter. Keep the login. Offer a live task instead, where you share your screen and play, or use a spare account that holds nothing you love.
