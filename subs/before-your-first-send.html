Step 1 of 6 What a tribute is, and what it doesn’t buy

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A tribute is a gift for a moment of play. You can’t enforce anything with it.
  • Before your first send, know what the money is, what your limit is and how you stop.
  • Set your limit while you’re calm, say it out loud, and let it reset on a fixed day.
  • Keep a floor you never touch. Sends come out of your limit only, and the rest stays in savings.
  • A real dom never needs your password, your screen or your address.

You will learn

  • What a tribute is and what it doesn’t buy.
  • How to set a limit, a floor and a stop before the evening starts.
  • How a wishlist pulls you up a ladder, and how to step off it.
  • Who can see you, and which requests are hard lines.
  • What to do if sending already feels like a loop.

If you can’t answer the three questions below tonight, wait. Nothing I offer is worth more than a calm hour spent on them.

  • Myth

    A big enough gift buys a place, a promise or a reply.

    Fact

    A gift buys the moment you send it. A dom may be kind, but nothing in the game can be enforced, and in practice you won’t get a refund. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I’m free to send nothing, so I’m not at risk.

    Fact

    On paper you’re free. In my experience most subs of a persona send something, so the real test is how a dom reacts the first time you say no. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Three things, and a limit that holds

Settle these before the evening

  1. 1

    The money. It’s a giftgiftAn item from a wishlist that the sub buys. It can also mean a reward image from the dom, and several gifts can be sold together as one bigger item. on a wishlist, and it pays for no service.

  2. 2

    The limitlimitA spending limit picked by the person who pays, while calm, before the session. It’s held outside your own head, in a server that enforces it, a bank limit or a trusted person, and it only goes up after a cooldown. A limit typed in the middle of a session is weak, and a limit is a ceiling, never a target.. It’s your number, set before the evening starts, and you don’t change it during the session.

  3. 3

    The stop. A way out that works at once and costs nothing.

A limit set in the middle of a session is weak. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Set it while you’re calm, and write it down.

Set a limit that holds

  1. 1

    Pick a monthly amount you can lose entirely. After it’s gone, every bill and every plan still has to stand.

  2. 2

    Fund it from money you saved beforehand. Never fund it by borrowing, and never add to it to see more.

  3. 3

    Pick a fixed reset day, and don’t refill early.

  4. 4

    Say it out loud to the dom.

  5. 5

    Make it real outside your own head: a separate account, a lower card limit, or someone you trust who knows the number.

  6. 6

    When you hit it, say so and wait for the reset.

Your floor and your budget

Your limit is monthly, and it’s the only money that may be sent. A floor is the amount you never touch at all. Everything else is surplus: it stays in savings and never counts toward sends. Raise the floor step by step as your income grows, so losing a job doesn’t turn into a crisis. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

How a wishlist pulls you up

A wishlist works like a price menu, and the dom steers you to the next rung. A ladder can run from a token amount to a very large one, so the first step is gentle and the way up is long. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Pick your rung from your limit, never from the page. Your limit is the most you’ll send, and you don’t have to reach it. Sending nothing is always the first option.

Who can see you, and the hard lines

Supporter names can show up on a public wishlist, and doms look at each other’s pages. You can also run into someone you know in a shared server. Use a handle you don’t mind several doms seeing, and keep a separate account for adult servers. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Physical gifts and billing break anonymity both ways. A dom who mails an item gives away her address, and a sub who gives his gives away his. Paying by card on a wishlist platform asks for a billing address, like any online purchase. The safe default is no addresses at all, or a neutral retail or forwarding service. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Red flags

Hard lines

  • Someone asks to see or control your screen, computer or phone.
  • Someone asks for a password, a login code or card details.
  • Someone asks you to install a tool from outside the store, or to give it access to every site.
  • Someone asks to control your bank account or to sign a debt contract.
  • Someone asks for your real name or home address.

Counter. Refuse, and stop sending. A real dom never needs any of it. Remote access is also how a lot of scams start.

Official advice says the same about remote access: don’t hand control of a computer or phone to a stranger. Check yourself

  1. Question 1 of 3When is the right time to set your limit?

    Answer. In a calm hour, before the evening. A limit typed in the moment is weak. Set it earlier, and make it real outside your own head.

  2. Question 2 of 3A dom asks when you get paid. What do you do?

    Answer. Decline, and say you send within your limit. Timing an ask to your pay or balance is targeting. You don’t owe her an answer.

  3. Question 3 of 3Someone offers to fix your tool by taking control of your screen. What do you do?

    Answer. Refuse, and stop sending. Nobody who plays fair needs access to your screen, your accounts or your codes.
