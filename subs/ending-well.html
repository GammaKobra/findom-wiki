Ending well

Last checked 3 November 20267 minutes to read

Anyone can leave, pause or change the terms, on either side of the screen. A good ending is short, kind and final. A crisis is something else, and it needs people outside the game.

Step 1 of 6 You can leave, and you can pause

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A sub can leave or pause at any time, and a good dom accepts it right away.
  • One short message makes a clean ending. Chasing, begging and guilt don’t.
  • A goodbye that hints at self-harm is a crisis. Call emergency services or a local helpline, and reach a person nearby.
  • A dom who’s paid can’t be the safety net for a sub in trouble.
  • Doms need pauses too, and a tool you can’t quit is a warning sign.

You will learn

  • How to leave, pause or end a session without damage.
  • Which pressures to spot and refuse when you leave.
  • How to cut down with a clean break and outside support.
  • What to do when someone leaves in crisis.
  • What a real exit looks like in a tool, and why doms burn out.

You don’t owe me a reason, an apology or one last send. If you go, I hope you get the life you want. If you ever tell me you’re in danger, I drop the character and send you to people who can help.

What a good ending looks like

A clean exit

  1. 1

    Say it in one line. Going silent is a way to cope, but a short message spares the other person the feeling of being ghosted.

  2. 2

    Thank the other person if you want to. You don’t have to.

  3. 3

    Stop. Don’t wait for permission, and don’t explain more than you want to.

  4. 4

    If you want a clean break, block the accounts you know, and ask the doms not to write.

A long silence is often read as an attempt to quit. The persona rule that fits here is never to chase: at most one short check-in with no invitation to come back, then the sub comes to her, and she doesn’t follow up. A sub who has said he’s quitting gets no message at all. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A sub has been silent for two weeks

You’re a dom, and a sub who wrote every day hasn’t said anything for two weeks.

When someone leaves, and the pressure to stay

A sub who leaves may get an appeal: apologies, promises to change, begging, questions about why, or a friendly third party sent to plead. For a sub, answering an appeal is optional, and a firm no ends it. For a dom, begging and go-betweens slide into pressure, so once a sub holds firm, she stops. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Red flags

When a goodbye is a crisis

  • He says he’s hurting himself, or wants to die.
  • He says goodbye in a way that hints at ending his life.
  • He’s deep in debt and sees no future.

Counter. Treat it as real. Drop the persona and the argument. Ask him to contact emergency services or a local helpline, and a person who’s physically near him.

If someone tells you they may hurt themselves

  1. 1

    Stop the scene and the argument. Drop the character and write plainly.

  2. 2

    Ask whether he’s safe right now, and listen.

  3. 3

    If he’s in danger, urge him to call his country’s emergency number now. Otherwise urge him to call a local helpline, listed in Where to get help.

  4. 4

    Ask him to reach a person who’s physically near, and to stay with that person.

  5. 5

    If you believe the danger is immediate and you know where he is, contact the emergency services for that place. If you’re in another country or don’t know where he is, a call may not go through. Use the platform’s self-harm report tool, look up that country’s police line on an official site, and don’t promise help you can’t give.

  6. 6

    Stay calm. Don’t make him responsible for your feelings, and don’t argue about money or the scene.

  7. 7

    You’re not a therapist, and you shouldn’t carry this alone. Tell someone you trust who knows you.

  8. 8

    Ask for nothing afterwards, and point him to outside help for the longer road. See When a sub is in trouble.

A dom is paid by the same sub, so she can’t be his safety net. A dom who earns from him can stop, listen, ask for nothing more and point him to outside help. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. If this is you, and you’re the one in danger, call your emergency number or a helpline now, tell someone near you, and send no money tonight.

The work has no closing time. For many people it sits beside a day job, with heavy emotional labour and little rest. Burnout is real. Stop rushing, stop beating yourself up, and step away from the screen. After a painful loss, remove the person, and read the timeline from a separate account so triggers don’t reach you.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Depending on a few people is fragile, because a main supporter can leave overnight. Two or three real relationships soften the damage, and a sub who announces he’s leaving hurts less than one who vanishes. Free chats and unpaid sessions lead to exhaustion, and a second dom who helps for free should get something back. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Check yourself

  1. Question 1 of 3A sub says goodbye in a way that hints at ending his life. What do you do?

    Answer. Drop the persona, urge emergency services or a helpline, and a person nearby. It’s a crisis. Outside help matters more than the scene.

  2. Question 2 of 3A sub asks to pause for a day. What should a dom do?

    Answer. Accept at once and ask for no apology. Pausing is fine, and guilt when someone leaves is pressure.

  3. Question 3 of 3Who is the right person to help a sub taper his spending?

    Answer. Someone who earns nothing from him. A paid person has a conflict of interest, and moving the money keeps the loop going.
