What a persona is

A persona is a character built and kept by someone. Her words are play. Your money, your time and your feelings are not.

In 30 seconds

  • A persona is a role. She is not a friend, an employee or a person you can check.

  • Who is behind her is unknown, so every claim about age, gender or place is unverified.

The hard lines of a persona

Limits belong in the persona file and not only in the head. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. One example reads: the persona never meets anyone, appears on camera or uses a voice. Nothing in her looks or setting is child-like, whatever the story says. She does not chase, and she never crosses a sub’s real limits. It is one example, and no model for every dom.

What not to share

Red flags

Giving access

  • You hand over a login as proof of trust.
  • You let her read your private conversations with others.
  • A chat stays open on a shared or unlocked computer.

Counter. Keep at least one account fully yours. If proof is asked, show only a send confirmation with names, balance and numbers hidden, never a login or an export of private chats. Change any password you shared. Lock the screen or close the chat.

A sub who gives a persona access to everything has no private space left, and in my experience he behaves differently when he knows he is watched. A login typed into a chat undoes security and anonymity at once, because anyone who can read the chat or the mailbox can use it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Keeping your head

Notice the loop early

  1. 1

    After a send, name what you feel: relief, regret, shame.

  2. 2

    Listen for the sentence “better this dom than a stranger”.

  3. 3

    If you want fewer sends, count the weeks since the last one.

  4. 4

    Tell one person you trust, soon after, not later.

  5. 5

    If you cannot stop, go to The spiral and Where to get help.

Regret paired with a reason to keep going is an early sign that spending is turning compulsive, and it is not a reason to feel safe. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
