Safe design

A tool that moves money inside a game is only safe if four things hold: a limit set in advance, a wait, a stop that works in every state, and a check from outside the page. This chapter explains each one and names the pressure designs to refuse.

In 30 seconds

  • A safe tool has four parts: a limit, a wait, a stop and a trusted check.
  • A limit held only by the page is no limit. It has to live where the tool can’t change it.
  • A stop must work in every state of the tool and must never cost anything.
  • Hidden totals, locks, paid exits, forced respins and sulking mascots are pressure designs to refuse.
  • Safe design lowers the risk. It doesn’t remove it. Someone in a spiral needs the tool off and help.

The limit and the wait

A limitlimitA spending limit picked by the person who pays, while calm, before the session. It’s held outside your own head, in a server that enforces it, a bank limit or a trusted person, and it only goes up after a cooldown. A limit typed in the middle of a session is weak, and a limit is a ceiling, never a target. that lives only in the page can be edited, reloaded or bypassed. The limit has to be read by the server, or better, from the payment provider itself. The daily and monthly limits the person chose are applied there. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A limit typed in the middle of a session is weak. Set it while you’re calm, before you start, and keep it somewhere you can’t change it in the moment: a server that enforces it, your bank, or a person you trust. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Count more than what’s done. A limit that counts only finished sends can be overshot by a send already on its way, so a tool counts what’s confirmed, what’s owed and the next step. A limit is a ceiling, never a target: an item that would push the total over it never gets picked. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Raise a limit only after a wait. Letting the person type any limit they like, with no maximum, is respectful but weak against an urge in the moment. A cooling-off delay before a limit goes up fixes that, and lowering a limit should always be instant. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Your bank is a brake you can keep on. An unusual run of late-night card payments can get you a call from the bank, and a confirmation on your phone for each payment slows spending down. A version of a tool sold as getting round that confirmation removes a brake you chose. Refuse it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A tool that keeps your signed-in session and a saved card, and buys in the background, takes away the pause you’d normally have between decisions. The only brake left is the limit you set beforehand. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A wait comes in three forms. One is a minimum time between two payments, enforced where the tool can’t skip it. Another is an acknowledgement timer: a command not accepted within a short window never runs. The third is an unwatched session that ends by itself. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Small amounts at short intervals still add up. Do the maths: USD 1.00 every 6 seconds is USD 600.00 in one hour, which is why an overnight mode needs a limit. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A stop that works in every state

The stop is visible, works at once, costs nothing and asks no question. Test it in every state: idle, waiting, in the middle of a payment, after a special mode, after an error. A build can lose its stop control after another control is pressed, so test the stop after every control. A drain you can’t stop breaks the basic promise of techdom. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Closing the window is a last resort, and it doesn’t count as a stop. Force-quitting always works, and you should know how to do it before you start, but a tool must not rely on it. A good tool fails by stopping. When something’s unclear, it never retries blindly. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • A bank verification appears: the tool waits for you and doesn’t click on. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • A payment isn’t confirmed by the platform: it isn’t counted. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • A payment went out without confirmation: the run stops and warns you. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A check from outside

The first meaning of a trusted check is the total. The amount counted comes from the payment provider’s own record, which the tool can’t write to. The sub’s browser never sets the price. A payment counts only when the processor confirms it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The second meaning is consent. The person accepts inside the program, and a controller can only ask. The web side can only make settings stricter. Everything starts switched off, with a required list of what’s allowed. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A quick exitquick exitA button, shortcut or menu entry that ends a tool or a remote session instantly and at no cost. Every tool that acts for a sub needs one that works in every state, and a tool without one isn’t safe. is always there: a keyboard shortcut, a button and a tray menu. A safety wordsafety wordA word agreed when consent is given that anyone can use to stop the play at once. It stops play between people, and the other person stops, answers calmly and doesn’t argue. is agreed when consent is given, so a session can end in a second. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Trust the design over the dom’s word. Whoever runs a control tool can act on your accounts at any moment, and this wiki doesn’t recommend control of accounts or machines in any design. Visible limits, an emergency stop and nothing that can act without you reduce that risk and never remove it. A dom who really uses consent tools shows them: a stop control, a record of what she can see, and a clear line between teasing and harm. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The dom can’t be your third party. She’s paid by the sends, so she can stop, listen and point you elsewhere, but she can’t manage your limits. Your bank, a person you trust, or a blocker you chose and can’t easily switch off can fill that role. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Keep secrets out of the tool. Keys and tokens go into a masked prompt in the owner’s own terminal, never into a chat, a commit or a screenshot. A safe tool holds no names, email addresses or card numbers, and any identifier it creates is random, never built from the computer name or the Windows account name. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

"use strict";

const { createGuard } = require("./guard");
const config = require("./config.json");

const MINUTE = 60;
const HOUR = 3600;

function usd(cents) {
  return "USD " + (cents / 100).toFixed(2);
}

// A stand-in for the outside source, such as the payment provider's own record.
// The guard can read it. Nothing in guard.js can write to it.
function makeProvider(startCents = 0) {
  let confirmed = startCents;
  return {
    read: () => confirmed,
    confirm: (cents) => {
      confirmed += cents;
    },
  };
}

function makeGuard(provider, overrides = {}) {
  return createGuard({ ...config, readConfirmedCents: provider.read, ...overrides });
}

function describe(result) {
  if (result.ok) return "allowed";
  return "refused (" + result.reason + (result.secondsLeft ? ", " + result.secondsLeft + " s left" : "") + ")";
}

console.log("1. The limit and the wait. Limit " + usd(config.limitCents) + ", wait " + config.waitSeconds / MINUTE + " minutes.");
{
  const provider = makeProvider();
  const guard = makeGuard(provider);
  const attempts = [
    [0, 2500],
    [1 * MINUTE, 2500],
    [16 * MINUTE, 2500],
    [32 * MINUTE, 2500],
    [48 * MINUTE, 2500],
    [64 * MINUTE, 2500],

      provider.confirm(amount);
      guard.release(amount);
    }

}

guard.js

"use strict";

// guard.js: the four-part rule in one small file.
//   1. A limit set in advance.
//   2. A wait between two sends, and a wait before the limit can go up.
//   3. A stop that works in every state.
//   4. A check outside the tool: the total comes from a source the tool cannot write.
// Money is whole cents and time is whole seconds. Nothing here touches a network or a payment.

function isCents(n) {
  return Number.isInteger(n) && n >= 0;
}

function createGuard({ limitCents, waitSeconds, raiseWaitSeconds, readConfirmedCents }) {
  if (!isCents(limitCents) || limitCents === 0) throw new RangeError("limitCents must be a whole number above zero");
  if (!isCents(waitSeconds) || !isCents(raiseWaitSeconds)) throw new RangeError("waits must be whole seconds");
  if (typeof readConfirmedCents !== "function") throw new TypeError("an outside total is required");

  const state = { limit: limitCents, raise: null, lastSendAt: null, reserved: 0, stopped: false };

  function applyRaise(now) {
    if (state.raise && now >= state.raise.at) {
      state.limit = state.raise.limit;
      state.raise = null;
    }
  }

  function outsideTotal() {
    try {
      const value = readConfirmedCents();
      return isCents(value) ? value : null;
    } catch (error) {
      return null;
    }
  }

  function refuse(reason, extra) {
    return Object.assign({ ok: false, reason }, extra);
  }

  return {
    // Ask before every send. A yes reserves the amount; nothing is sent by this file.
    ask(amountCents, now) {
      if (state.stopped) return refuse("stopped");
      if (!Number.isInteger(amountCents) || amountCents <= 0) return refuse("bad-amount");
      applyRaise(now);
      const confirmed = outsideTotal();
      if (confirmed === null) return refuse("outside-total-unreadable");
      if (confirmed + state.reserved + amountCents > state.limit) return refuse("limit");
      if (state.lastSendAt !== null && now - state.lastSendAt < waitSeconds) {
        return refuse("wait", { secondsLeft: waitSeconds - (now - state.lastSendAt) });
      }
      state.reserved += amountCents;
      state.lastSendAt = now;
      return { ok: true };
    },

    // Call once the outside source shows the payment. Until then the amount counts twice, which errs on the safe side.
    release(amountCents) {
      state.reserved = Math.max(0, state.reserved - amountCents);
    },

    // Lowering the limit is immediate. Raising it only starts a clock.
    setLimit(newLimitCents, now) {
      applyRaise(now);
      if (!isCents(newLimitCents) || newLimitCents === 0) return { ok: false, reason: "bad-limit" };
      if (newLimitCents <= state.limit) {
        state.limit = newLimitCents;
        state.raise = null;
        return { ok: true, effective: "now" };
      }
      state.raise = { limit: newLimitCents, at: now + raiseWaitSeconds };
      return { ok: true, effective: "after-wait", at: state.raise.at };
    },

    // No argument, no question, no way to fail. It also cancels any raise that is waiting.
    stop() {
      state.stopped = true;
      state.raise = null;
    },

    // The running total, always readable.
    status(now) {
      applyRaise(now);
      const confirmed = outsideTotal();
      return {
        limitCents: state.limit,
        confirmedCents: confirmed,
        reservedCents: state.reserved,
        stopped: state.stopped,
        raiseWaiting: state.raise !== null,
      };
    },
  };
}

module.exports = { createGuard };

Before a tool reaches a person

A wrong build can charge a real person a different amount than the wheel shows, and a test switch that skips the payment page, left in a build, is a risk. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. These steps are for makers, and they’re also a list of questions for anyone about to install.

A release routine

  1. 1

    Label every build test or real. A test build that really takes money must say so.

  2. 2

    Remove every switch that skips the payment page.

  3. 3

    Check the file you actually ship by comparing its size or hash with the one you tested.

  4. 4

    Show the amount displayed next to the amount charged.

  5. 5

    Rehearse end to end: a fake provider first, the provider’s test mode next, and one tiny agreed real payment last.

  6. 6

    If the tool misbehaves, pull it from live sessions until it’s fixed and retested.

Check yourself

  1. Question 1 of 3Where should the running total come from?

    Answer. The payment provider’s record, which the tool can’t write to. A total held by the tool can be wrong, edited or reset. The tool can’t change an outside record.

  2. Question 2 of 3When can a limit go up?

    Answer. Only after a wait, while lowering it stays instant. A raise made in the heat of the moment is the weak point. A wait moves that decision to a calm time.

  3. Question 3 of 3A tool offers a top tier that locks its master switch. What does safe design say?

    Answer. Refuse it, because a safe tool keeps one visible, free way out. A lock leaves force-quit as the only way out. A tool must not depend on that.
