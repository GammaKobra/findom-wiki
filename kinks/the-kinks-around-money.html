This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A money kink is play where giving money, losing it or handing over control of it is part of the pleasure.
  • Most of these kinks are ordinary adult play with a script. What makes them different is that the money is real.
  • You can refuse words, labels and vows. A decent dom drops them and moves on.
  • Some designs are built to make stopping hard. Learn to spot them from the side of the person who pays.
  • Debt isn’t play. If a game needs borrowed money, it isn’t a kink anymore.

  • What counts as a money kink, and what each common one involves.

  • Which pressure devices to recognise and refuse.
  • How consent is checked inside a scene, and how to refuse a label.

Each of these is play between adults who agreed to it. What sets this group apart is that the money is real, and so is the person paying. Read every entry with that in mind.

Ownership and possession

Ownership talk is common: fake jealousy when you talk to other doms, vows of loyalty, lines saying you belong to nobody else. As roleplay it’s a kink. The same sentences can also put you off comparing doms, or off building a life that doesn’t revolve around one persona. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The words are the same either way. What changes is who gains, and whether you can walk away.

Red flags

A chain you can’t see

  • She won’t say who gets what she collects.
  • A senior persona keeps showing up in her story and on her wishlist.
  • You’re asked to send to another persona for her.

Counter. Ask where the money ends up before you send. A vague answer counts as a no, and you choose your own amount.
