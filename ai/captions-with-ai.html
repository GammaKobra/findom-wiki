Step 1 of 7 Show it the voice first

In 30 seconds

  • Give the model a short voice sheet and three real captions before you ask for anything.
  • Ask for several drafts, keep one, and rewrite it in your own words.
  • Cut what gives a machine away: ad words, stray hashtags, every line built the same way.
  • Suggestive captions are fine on most platforms. Explicit content and selling for sends are where the rules bite.
  • A sub’s name, messages and amounts never go into a chat with a model.

A model can write a hundred captions a minute. None of them is yours until you’ve cut it down and said it your way.

Show it the voice first

With no instructions, a model writes like an ad. So before you ask for a caption, give it a voice sheet: a few lines on who the persona is and how she talks. In my experience, ten lines work better than two pages, because the model follows a short sheet closely and a long one loosely. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A voice sheet in five parts

  1. 1

    Who she is, in one line: her name, her look, the kind of dom she plays.

  2. 2

    How she talks: how long her sentences are, words she uses, words she never uses.

  3. 3

    How she treats the reader: teasing, cold, warm or mocking.

  4. 4

    Three captions you’ve posted and liked, pasted as they are.

  5. 5

    What she never writes: explicit sex, real names, promises about money.

The examples do more work than the description. A model picks up rhythm from three real captions far better than from words like “playful” or “dominant”. Swap the examples every few weeks, or every draft starts to sound like the same three posts. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Most of the sheet can come straight from your persona file, as Build a persona explains.

Ask for ten, keep one

Ask for ten drafts at once, each under a set length, and pick the best. Choosing is faster than fixing one bad draft. Say what the post is for: a new picture, a wishlist item, a tool launch, a quiet day. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Make it sound like you

Every draft gets a human pass before it goes out. Read it out loud, and if you wouldn’t say it, change it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Then check it still says one clear thing, as Posting and workflows explains.

What gives a machine away

  • Red flag 1Ad words: “exciting”, “amazing”, “don’t miss”, “check out”.
  • Red flag 2Hashtags and emoji you didn’t ask for.
  • Red flag 3Every line built the same way, often in threes.
  • Red flag 4A question tacked onto the end of every post.
  • Red flag 5A long word where she’d use a short one.
  • Red flag 6The same opener day after day.

Keep a note of the changes you make most often. After a month, add them to the voice sheet, so the model stops making the same mistakes. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Keep the sub out of it

Replies are where AI help gets risky, because the material belongs to someone else. A sub’s messages, name, handle, photos and amounts are his. Pasting them into a chat hands them to a company he never agreed to. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Help with a reply, without his data

  1. 1

    Describe the situation in your own words: “a sub asks for a bigger task than the one agreed”.

  2. 2

    Write “he” or “the sub” instead of his name or handle.

  3. 3

    Leave out amounts, or round them.

  4. 4

    Never paste screenshots of the chat.

  5. 5

    Ask for three short replies in the persona’s voice, then rewrite the one you pick.

Write prices, totals and dates yourself. A model can get a number wrong, and in a money message the mistake is yours. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Some replies shouldn’t be drafted by a model at all. If a sub says money is short, mentions debt or sounds unwell, stop the money part and answer him yourself, in plain words. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See When a sub is in trouble.
