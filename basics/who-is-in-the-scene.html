Step 1 of 6 Five kinds of people

In 30 seconds

  • The scene has five kinds of actors, and one person often plays several.
  • It’s small. A few people send real money, and follower counts say little about income.
  • Doms are also fans, customers and subs of each other, and some teach newer doms.
  • Work done for a dom can be a fair swap or free labour. The terms decide which.
  • Labels, rank ladders and talk of ownership are play when chosen and pressure when they trap you.

You will learn

  • Who the five kinds of actors are and what each one wants.
  • What a small server looks like and what each room is for.
  • How doms deal with each other, and what to agree first.
  • Which pressure patterns to recognise and refuse.
  • What to do when someone says goodbye in a way that frightens you.

The scene is smaller and more tangled than it looks from outside. Most of what I know comes from one corner of it, so there’s a lot this page can’t cover. Everyone in it is an adult, or has to be.

What a small server looks like

Most servers are small, with a handful of active members. Opening up wider brings in scam doms and costs time, so a lot of hosts stay small. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The numbers are small

VVivian’s field notes

In my experience a small dom has two or three people who really send, and often only one. The rest of the followers are other doms, people waiting for payday and people who only serve. I have a stake in the money, like every dom who gets paid. Because a few payers carry so much, losing one is a big deal for a small dom, and that gives her a reason to want him back. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. A sub should notice that, and stick to his limit.

Lots of small doms run with two or three subs and no server of their own. A scene that small runs on reputation, and reputation runs on hearsay. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Check what you hear, and talk to the person before you believe a story about her.

Dom to dom

Doms deal with each other all the time. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Each of these needs something agreed first.

  • Mentoring. An experienced dom teaches a newer one, often in a training room. Agree when she steps in and when you try alone. The friction often comes from her stepping in without explaining. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • Referrals. Doms send each other to specialists for payments, code or art. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Agree what gets passed on, and keep private details private.
  • Collabs. Two characters share a story. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Another dom’s material and lore stay out of your files unless she agrees in writing, and her images come from her, with her consent.
  • Hierarchies. A betadommebetadommeA junior or second dom who works beside an established one, in some communities as a helper or trainee. The role isn’t worth much to the junior unless there’s a clear reason like training or experience, and it needs agreed terms like any unpaid work. The word also names a kink where a domme is made to lose to a stronger dom. is a dom persona who’s submissive to a senior dom, and she may pass up part of what her own subs send. A faildommefaildommeA persona that plays a failing dom who gets beaten, drained or humiliated by a stronger one. It’s a hashtag and a persona type, and also a loose label for a dom who falls short of the role. chain is a pyramid where money moves up and humiliation is the point. The role isn’t worth much to the junior unless it comes with a clear reason, like events or training. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
  • Sharing a sub. Two doms can share a sub if they tell him it’s a team and agree how sends are split. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. A sub who’s handed over, passed around or ranked hasn’t agreed to it unless he said so.
  • Rivalry. Copying ideas is common. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Studying another maker’s work is a different thing from copying it. Trying to get a rival’s payment account closed harms her and everyone paid through that account, and that isn’t competing.

Two doms can agree to play together, with one in the role of sub. One dom trying to get money out of another dom who hasn’t agreed to that role isn’t play. Talk of ownership needs the same care, because a sub is a person who chooses and ownership talk can blur that. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Service is a kind of currency in the scene. A gen slavegen slaveIn some communities, a sub who serves by making generated images for a dom’s posts. The same fairness rule applies as for any unpaid work: he picks the scope, he’s free to refuse and he gets thanked. makes images, a code slavecode slaveA sub who writes software for a dom as his way of serving, instead of paying or on top of it. It’s fair only when he chose the scope, can say no without losing his place and gets thanked. Otherwise it’s free labour dressed up as devotion. builds tools, and some subs only make edits. It can be a fair swap, and it can be free labour dressed up as devotion. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When a sub says money is short, the money part stops for that evening. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The dom doesn’t offer anything to replace the money that evening. If he suggests work himself, later and with terms, the four questions below apply. If the trouble is serious, see Where to get help and When a sub is in trouble.

Four questions before you say yes

  1. 1

    What exactly is the job, and when does it end?

  2. 2

    Can I say no later without losing my place?

  3. 3

    Who owns what I make, and will my name stay on it?

  4. 4

    Am I thanked, and is any money or other return part of the deal?

  • Myth

    Work for a dom is always free labour.

    Fact

    It can be a fair swap when the person doing it chooses the scope and can refuse. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Work for a dom is always a fair swap.

    Fact

    It turns into free labour when the scope grows, saying no costs him his place, or he’s never thanked. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Pressure patterns to recognise

Labels, ranks and loyalty games are play between adults who chose them. They turn into pressure when you can’t leave, compare or stop without a penalty. When someone says goodbye in a way that frightens you

Any group built on attachment will have members in distress. Researchers report that findommes met clients’ verbal aggression and, in some cases, suicidal tendencies. A goodbye that hints at self-harm is a crisis, even when it looks like a normal exit. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

If a goodbye frightens you

  1. 1

    Take it seriously, whether he talks about ending his life or hurting himself.

  2. 2

    Don’t answer it with a scene, with anger or with talk of money.

  3. 3

    If he might be in danger right now, call the emergency number where he is. If you know where that is, tell them.

  4. 4

    Point him to a crisis line in his country and to a person near him. See Where to get help.

  5. 5

    Tell someone you trust that it happened. Don’t share his name or his messages with the scene.

  6. 6

    Afterwards, rest. A dom who’s paid by a sub can’t be his safety net. Outside help is the way out.
