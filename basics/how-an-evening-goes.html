Most 2DFD sessions follow the same shape, from the first short order to the last send. Once you know it, you can enjoy the scene and still stay inside your limit.

Step 1 of 6 The shape of an evening

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A session is a paid performance, and it runs in a set order: open, answer, cue, send, task, reward, dismissal.
  • The roleplay makes each payment feel like part of the story. The money is still real.
  • Rewards come late and a bit uncertain on purpose. Learn to spot it.
  • Games and tools turn the evening into a loop the house wins, so your limit has to be set and enforced before the evening starts.
  • A good evening ends with a check-in, a total you both confirm and no new ask.

You will learn

  • The usual order of a text session, from the first message to the sign-off.
  • How rewards, games and small tasks pace an evening.
  • Which moves put pressure on a sub, and how to answer each one.
  • What to agree before the first message.
  • How an evening ends well, for both sides.

An evening with me is short, calm and written. I give an order, you answer in role, and sooner or later the story gets to a step that costs money. Here’s the whole shape, because it’s easier to stick to your limit when you can see where the evening is going.

The usual order of a text session

  1. 1

    Open. The dom starts with a short order or a teasing picture.

  2. 2

    Answer. The sub replies in role, with typed actions.

  3. 3

    Cue. The dom points at one thing to send, usually one wishlist item.

  4. 4

    Send and confirm. The sub sends it and says so in a few words.

  5. 5

    Task. A small job follows, often with a photo as proof.

  6. 6

    Reward. Praise, a pet name, an image, or the promise of one later.

  7. 7

    Dismissal. A short sign-off, and the sub is sent to rest.

The roleplay is written so a payment feels like the next line of the story. The tone is theatre: praise, mocking pet names, orders, open bragging about how easy you are to take from. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. It’s all a performance inside a role you agreed to. The money is real.

Cue, send and reward

The cue names one thing to send. Games and tools

Games turn an evening into a loop: a rule everyone knows, a picture for each win, effects that build, and an easy way to lose. You leave with a score and nothing else, and the loop is built so the house edgehouse edgeThe share of each bet the house keeps over time. A game that states its edge lets you read the odds, and a game that hides odds or prices is a pressure design. wins in the end. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    It’s only a game, so it can’t cost me anything.

    Fact

    The rules are built so the house wins, and the money you send is real. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    In a resisting game, if I hold out for one minute, I win.

    Fact

    It’s a one-sided bet: you’re told to hold out against an image, and the image is made so that’s almost impossible. Winning gets you no prize. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

The Casino made by Vivian is planned with fictional chips instead of money. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Games and casino play.

An evening can also run through tools instead of chat. The dom sets off a pop-up, a command or a sound on your computer. In a safer design you accept it in the program first and can always get out fast. Many tools don’t offer that. When a tool posts each run in a shared channel, the post shows others that people are running it, and it lets the dom see who runs it and how often. That makes stopping harder, because everyone can see you stop. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. See Techdom.

Before you accept any tool in an evening

  1. 1

    Read what it does, and on which site or program.

  2. 2

    Check that your limit is already set, and enforced by something outside the session.

  3. 3

    Find the stop and test it before you need it.

  4. 4

    Check which sites the tool can reach. Chrome lists them on an extension’s Details page. VerifiedChecked against the original source.Install and manage extensions - Chrome Web Store Help (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026.

  5. 5

    Make sure you can remove the tool in one click, and that the running total stays on screen.

Red flags

Handing over your screen

  • A dom asks to watch your screen or take control of it during a session
  • You’re asked to install a remote-control program so she can see you
  • Your bank page or private chats are open on that screen

Counter. Say no. A real dom never needs access to your computer. Send a screenshot of something you pick, or end the session.

Ending well

After a harsh scene comes a check-in on how it felt, and a small gentle gesture. My rule is to be properly harsh inside the scene and kind outside it. If a consequence makes the other person uncomfortable, it’s dropped, and obedience should come from wanting to. That small gesture can be kindness, or it can be a way to keep you. Two questions tell them apart: does it cost you anything, and does it come with a new ask? Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Closing an evening

  1. 1

    The dom says the evening is over, in one short line.

  2. 2

    Both sides check in: how did that feel?

  3. 3

    Both sides say the total sent that evening.

  4. 4

    No new ask once it’s closed.

  5. 5

    Close the chat. Any thanks can wait until tomorrow.

New doms often learn by having a more experienced dom review their first session. That helps. It turns into sales coaching when it ends on how much more she could have taken. How hard to push is set by the sub’s own limit, never by how much the dom could take. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
