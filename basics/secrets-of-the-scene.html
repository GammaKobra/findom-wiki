Basics Chapter 7

Secrets of the scene

Last checked 3 November 20268 minutes to read

The real secrets of 2DFD are mostly structure. A few people pay, attention is scarce, reach is partly luck, and ideas are copied within weeks.

VerifiedChecked against the original source.ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet.

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Most of the scene’s money comes from very few people, and those people come and go.
  • Warmth is part of a business that needs retention. It can be sincere and still steer your spending.
  • Hours of work do not buy reach, and no rule for staying visible has been checked.
  • Many drawn personas are said to be run by someone other than the character, often a man. Nobody has counted.
  • A serious dom shows her frame before she asks for money, and never needs a workaround.

You will learn

  • Why income rests on a handful of people, and why subs leave.
  • What decides who is seen, and which rules are only folklore.
  • How to read a persona’s claims about who she is.
  • Which designs and pressure tactics to recognise and refuse.
  • What a serious dom shows before she asks for anything.

I will tell you what the scene keeps quiet, and it is less glamorous than the rumours. Most of it is structure: who pays, who is seen, who copies whom. Read it before you start, as a sub or as a dom, and you will know where the ground is soft.

Money lives in a few hands

In my experience, a persona’s income rests on very few people. One supporter can carry a third of a good week or more, and a long follower list can hold only a handful of people who reliably send. A new persona’s followers are often other doms who chat but do not spend. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A leaderboardleaderboardA ranking of subs by how much they sent or how far they got, kept by the dom and sometimes shown in public. A public tally turns spending into a contest, which is a pressure design to notice, so a name should go on one only by choice. shows this and also hides it. One sub with two accounts can hold two of the top places, and a board that ranks by the number of gifts puts a flood of tiny sends above a larger earner. A public tally of wins is advertising and proves nothing. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Subs come and go. They leave for a rival, from boredom, curiosity or regret over choices made while aroused. They send every few days or in waves, not daily, and many pay several doms at once. Some vanish after a large send or a relapserelapseSending money again after deciding to stop. A relapser is a sub who quits and returns several times, and it is a real warning sign of compulsive spending. One press commentator compared some cases to problem gambling. Telling someone is a first step, and Where to get help lists where to go., and some return months later. A dom cannot stop anyone leaving, only make staying more appealing. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Read this as a sub. A business that lives on a few people has every reason to keep each of them close. The warmth can still be sincere. The test is whether the performance is steering your spending, and in my experience the person who pays most is the one under the most pressure. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Reach is a lottery

Who posts matters more than what is posted. A post is shown to a few people and shown further if they react. A weaker post from a favoured account can outperform a better one from an unfavoured one. A repost from a bigger account can lift a small one briefly. Early views say little: a post can idle then rise. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Hours of editing do not buy reach. A plain picture with one word sometimes flies, and a long edit sometimes sits at a few views. A brand-new account is often pushed hard at first, and an older one with poor visibility is treated as dead. An account labelled or hidden from suggestions with an SSBSSBShort for search suggestion ban on the main social platform: the account stops appearing in search suggestions, so reach drops and growth of a new persona suffers. The expansion is inferred from how the word is used and not from a platform document. Checking for it every day can be demoralising. loses most of its reach. What wins, and who is behind it

Simple and new tends to beat polished. The tools that do best are often a simple idea nobody bothered to build. A recognisable character and easy setup count as much as the art. Ideas are copied within weeks, and since AI writes most of the code, nothing technical stops a copy. What a copy cannot take is the audience of the account that promotes the tool. Copying a mechanic is common, and the scene dislikes copies without credit. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Harming a creator because you dislike her business is no justification.

A drawn persona scales in a way a photographed one cannot, because pictures, voice lines and lore can be made in bulk. A fixed visual recipe makes her readable at thumbnail size. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Many drawn personas are run by someone other than the character, often a man. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. It is commonly said and nobody has counted it. Attention, burnout and the first send

Attention is the scarce resource. A dom’s day is split between incoming messages, posting, the community and outside duties. Chatting without sending can still use up a dom’s day. Protect your attention

  1. 1

    Set a daily cap on time for contacts who do not send, before the day starts. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  2. 2

    Tell free chatters that you are busy, and do not undersell your time.

  3. 3

    Show your frame first: limits, prices and a way to stop.

  4. 4

    When stress takes over, pause and say so. A pause needs no drama.

Pressure you may meet

Some designs look like fun. Slot games with deliberate near-misses, bets that rise as the effects grow and bets with no prize for winning are standard devices. Gacha mechanics are copied from commercial games, with rarity stars, pity counterspityA counter in gacha games that guarantees a top reward after a fixed number of pulls. Scene versions copy the idea, and a guarantee set far away is a reason to keep pulling., banners and farming loops. The dom who builds them knows what they do. Red flags

Designs that look like fun

  • The reel stops one step short of the prize, again and again.
  • A bet has no prize at all for winning.
  • A tool copies a pity counter and banners from a commercial game.
  • A lock can be removed only by buying something.

Counter. Close it and say stop. Do the sum in Numbers before you send anything. A free stop that always works is the minimum.

Tactics, and how to refuse them
What they say or doThe counter
You are special, I care about all my subs, you do not have to sendJudge a dom by what she asks and rewards in practice. If everyone sends, the reassurance is decoration.
I could make you send a fortune with one word, and I will not, because I careThe power exists whether or not it is used. Trust built this way is dependence.
The doms you paid before are losersRate a dom by how she treats you, not by how she rates others.
Praise, titles and new projects for work you do for freeAgree the scope in advance, and keep the right to say no without losing your place.
A short firm command, a finished example that makes it look easy, then the same command again when you hesitateHesitation is information. Take the night, and decide in your own time.
Hand over your public account so it can show your sendsNever. It is your identity, not a game feature.

These lines sit in the scene’s toolkit. They are listed so that you can see them coming.

Scene politics reach subs. Feuds spill into subs’ accounts, and a call-out can be sabotage by proxy. A rumour can spread private screenshots to dozens of people before anyone checks. Supporter names can be seen on public wishlists, and friend doms look at each other’s pages. Assume that the name you use is recognised across the scene. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What a serious dom shows

A serious dom shows her frame before asking for money: limits, what she throws out, the plain truth about prices and a visible way to stop. She tells a sub ahead of time when he must not send, and what follows. She forwards misdirected money at once and says so. She checks availability before she promises a gift. A dom who hides these has told you what she is. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Whatever a sub says about his money, a serious dom does not use it to ask for more. I have heard the argument that most subs who say they are broke are lying. The cost of being wrong falls on him. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Some makers hide what a payment page is. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. I do not describe how any of it is built. A processor that catches this can freeze the funds, including a sub’s money. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. Your bank saying no is an answer. A real dom never needs a workaround.

If any of this sounds familiar, set a cap in a calm hour, keep a stop that works at once, and wait before you raise either. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. If sending already feels hard to stop, read The spiral and Where to get help.

Check yourself

  1. Question 1 of 2A dom’s follower list is long. What does it tell you about her income?

    Answer. Very little, because followers can be other doms or people who never send. Income rests on a handful of people, and a long list can hide how few they are.

  2. Question 2 of 2A dom says she could make you send a fortune with one word, and that she will not because she cares. What do you do?

    Answer. Notice that the power is there whether or not she uses it. Power followed by visible restraint builds trust and dependence together.
