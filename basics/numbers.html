Step 1 of 6 What is measured and what is not

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Nothing in the 2DFD scene has been measured. Every income figure is memory, a guess or a rumour.
  • The usual case is small: very few subs really send, and one of them can bring in most of the money.
  • The price of one item hides the total. Multiply before you send.
  • Fixed fees decide which payment rail pays off, and tax changes what a share really costs.
  • Big figures are the bait. Read them last, and as hearsay.

You will learn

  • Which numbers are measured, documented, self-reported or hearsay.
  • What a small persona’s numbers look like.
  • How to compute a break-even, a real price and an hourly rate.
  • Which figures to distrust.

Here are the numbers, and how little most of them are worth. The small ones are common and nobody talks about them. The big ones are loud and mostly rumour. If a number can’t be traced back to a source, treat it as a story.

The small numbers

In my experience, the real numbers are tiny. A small persona has very few subs who really send. Here “active” means sending, not chatting. A long follower list can hold only a handful of people who pay regularly. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Income comes from a few people and arrives unevenly. One sub can make up a large share of a small persona’s income. A strong week can be very good, and other days bring little or nothing. The biggest single sends are rare. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What it costs to run a persona

A persona costs money before she earns any. Image and video generation, subscriptions, hosting and a domain add up, and one paying sub can’t cover the tools. Some say a dom is doing fine when the sends easily cover her subscriptions. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. That only counts the dom’s costs, and leaves out what the sub spent.

Compute a break-even before you choose a rail

  1. 1

    Write down the processor’s fixed yearly fee, F.

  2. 2

    Write down the commission rates: p for the processor and c for the platform.

  3. 3

    The break-even is F divided by (c minus p), in yearly sales.

  4. 4

    Made-up example: F is USD 1,500.00, p is 10 percent and c is 20 percent. The break-even is USD 1,500.00 divided by 0.10, which is USD 15,000.00 a year. Below that, the platform costs less.

  5. 5

    Compare it with your honest yearly estimate, never with your best month.

Tax changes what a percentage costs you. In many countries this kind of income is probably taxable, but the rules vary, so ask a professional. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. The maths is simple. Say tax takes 40 percent, so you keep 60 percent. A 15 percent share promised on the gross is then 25 percent of what you keep. A cost of USD 300.00 a month needs USD 500.00 in sends.

Do the sum

  1. Question 1 of 3A design guarantees its top reward after 80 pulls at USD 3.00 a pull. What’s the real price?

    Answer. USD 240.00. You only reach the guarantee after the whole run: 80 times USD 3.00.

  2. Question 2 of 3A tool sends USD 1.00 every 6 seconds. What does it send in an hour?

    Answer. USD 600.00. 3,600 seconds divided by 6 is 600 sends.

  3. Question 3 of 3A board ranks doms by the number of gifts. What does the top place show?

    Answer. The most gifts received, which tiny sends can inflate. Ranking by count says little about income. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Reach, time and asking

Measure your reach instead of guessing it. It varies a lot between accounts of the same creator. In my experience, for a small account the first hour of an ordinary post brings a few hundred views, a typical post one to two thousand, and a lucky spike eight to ten thousand. A repost from a bigger account is often the only boost. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Time is the other budget. A persona working part-time can handle far fewer subs than one working full time. Fast releases hide the preparation: someone who ships often has probably prepared for months. Drawn content can be made in bulk: a local setup can produce hundreds of pictures, while weak hardware takes minutes for one. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Asking is what moves the money most, and you’re the one being asked. A dom who answers nearly everyone earns more than one who skips sessions she could take. Asking again and again is how a pressure tactic works, and you can say no every time. If you’re asked again after you said stop, that’s the tactic, and Pressure tactics, both ways names it. Timing an ask to your payday or balance, or putting a deadline on your decision, is the same tactic. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
