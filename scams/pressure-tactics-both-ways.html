Scams Chapter 4

Pressure tactics, both ways

Last checked 3 November 20267 minutes to read

Pressure is a set of moves that make a no feel costly. This chapter names them from the side of the person who must recognise and refuse them, whether the move comes from a dom, from a sub or from a design.

VerifiedChecked against the original source.ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Pressure takes away time, choice or privacy. The moves are few and they repeat.
  • Some of these moves are play between adults who chose them. The line is whether you chose, with a cap and a stop you control.
  • Words about respect do not mean the pressure has stopped.
  • Pressure also lives in games and apps, with no person needed.
  • It runs both ways, but not equally: the dom is paid and designs the tools.

You will learn

  • How to spot the moves that take away your time, your feelings or your privacy.
  • A short counter for each move.
  • Which designs in games and apps are pressure.
  • What subs do to doms, and how written rules answer it.
  • How to refuse once, calmly, and where to go when the spending stops being a game.

Pressure is the part of this game that works best, and it works on anyone. I name each move from the side of the person who receives it, so that it can be refused. Some of these moves are play between adults who chose them. What separates play from pressure is whether you chose, with a cap and a stop that you control.

How pressure works

Pressure takes away time, choice or privacy. Nearly every move below does one of the three, and it works best on a person who is aroused, tired, ashamed or attached. A scene built on those states is a scene where they are normal, which is why the same moves work here so well. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A sub tells you he is spending heavily. A senior dom offers to help and suggests that the two of you share him, with a split of what he sends.

Pressure from the sub side

Subs pressure doms too. Press reports that submissives also try to scam dominatrices. ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.Financial Domination Has a Scamming Problem (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026. The moves are plainer: haggling, “just this once”, a false claim to belong to another dom, a technical excuse at the moment more is asked, and vanishing after the first send. A dom who has written her rules down can point to them instead of arguing. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Whatever a sub says about money, the dom does not rule on whether it is true. Any claim that money is short pauses the money part of the evening. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Most subs who say they are broke are lying, so there is room to ask for more.

    Fact

    Nobody can tell from outside, and the cost of being wrong falls on the sub. When a sub says money is short, the money part of the evening stops. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    If I do not take everything from an unstable sub, someone else will.

    Fact

    That is the justification of exploitation. It moves the blame for harm onto the person who is hurt. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Write your rules down

  1. 1

    State before the first session what you do not do: haggling, free previews, bargains.

  2. 2

    Say plainly, ahead of time, when a sub must not send, and that you will stop the session and refuse the send if he does.

  3. 3

    When a rule is tested, point to it. Do not argue.

  4. 4

    When a sub says money is short, pause the money part of the evening.

  5. 5

    When a sub is caught lying about another dom, block rather than spend energy on it. A firm no ends it, and stop messaging when someone asks.

Saying no

Refuse once, calmly

  1. 1

    Say the boundary once, in one sentence.

  2. 2

    Do not explain, justify or prove it.

  3. 3

    Wait before answering anything that carries a deadline.

  4. 4

    If the pressure continues after the no, that is the answer. Leave, block, keep the messages and tell one person you trust.

Name the lever

  1. Question 1 of 3A dom thanks you for a send in a lukewarm way and hints that you can obviously afford more. What is it?

    Answer. A sales line that tries to raise the amount. The line praises and shames in one breath. Set the amount before the evening.

  2. Question 2 of 3A dom says she respects your limits, then asks for a small test just past them. What do you do?

    Answer. Take the respect and ignore the next sentence. Words about limits do not mean the nudge has stopped.

  3. Question 3 of 3A dom gives permission but says she will just be sad. What is the cost?

    Answer. A permission that arrives with guilt attached. Ask for a plain yes or no, and talk about the need underneath.

When the spending stops being a game

Some signs say that a sub is deep in compulsion: spending hundreds in one day, paying several doms at once, weeks of chatting without calming down, deleting chats after each send. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Hiding the spending from a partner is a risk factor, and one author compares some cases to problem gambling. ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.The Psychology of Financial Dominatrixes (blog post) (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026. Compulsion needs slower handling, not extra asks. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A dom who earns from a sub cannot be his safety net. She can stop, listen, ask for nothing more and point outward. Tapering his spending herself or moving him to a better dom is a conflict of interest, not help. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. In a crisis, the help is outside: emergency services, a helpline, a person physically near. Go to The spiral, Breaks that hold, When a sub is in trouble and Where to get help.
