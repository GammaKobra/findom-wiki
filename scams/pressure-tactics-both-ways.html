Scams Chapter 4

Pressure tactics, both ways

Last checked 3 November 20267 minutes to read

Pressure is any move that makes saying no feel costly. This chapter names those moves from the side of the person who has to spot and refuse them, whether they come from a dom, a sub or a design.

Step 1 of 7 How pressure works

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • Pressure takes away time, choice or privacy. The moves are few and they repeat.
  • Some of these moves are play between adults who chose them. The line is whether you chose, with a limit and a stop you control.
  • Words about respect don’t mean the pressure has stopped.
  • Pressure also lives in games and apps, with no person behind it.
  • It runs both ways, but not equally: the dom gets paid and designs the tools.

You will learn

  • How to spot the moves that take away your time, your feelings or your privacy.
  • A short counter for each move.
  • Which designs in games and apps are pressure.
  • What subs do to doms, and how written rules answer it.
  • How to refuse once, calmly, and where to go when the spending stops being a game.

Pressure is the part of this game that works best, and it works on anyone.

Pressure built into games and apps

Between doms, and on makers

Pressure also runs between doms, and between doms and the people who build for them.

Between doms and on makers
What they say or doThe counter
A story about your sub: “he isn’t really yours”A sub is a person who makes his own choices. Ask him, and ask the dom concerned. Never act on a rumour alone. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
An offer to help with a sub who spends heavily, and later the split has changed without youDon’t share a heavy spender between doms: that’s a red flag. Pause the money, point him to a limit and outside help, and agree any other arrangement in numbers and in writing before it starts. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
A mentor’s trump card: “after all I did for you”, “then I’m leaving”Keep business apart from emotional debt. Ask for the reasoning, and refuse arrangements where seniority replaces an explanation. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
Praise and pet names in exchange for unlimited free workAgree on scope and payment first, and keep the right to refuse. Otherwise it’s free labour dressed up as devotion. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Pressure from the sub side

Subs pressure doms too. Press reports that submissives also try to scam dominatrices. ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.Financial Domination Has a Scamming Problem (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026. Their moves are simpler: haggling, “just this once”, a false claim to belong to another dom, a technical excuse right when more is asked, and vanishing after the first send. A dom who’s written her rules down can point to them instead of arguing. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Whatever a sub says about money, the dom doesn’t get to decide whether it’s true. Any claim that money is short pauses the money part of the evening. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Most subs who say they’re broke are lying, so there’s room to ask for more.

    Fact

    Nobody can tell from outside, and the cost of being wrong falls on the sub. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    If I don’t take everything from an unstable sub, someone else will.

    Fact

    That’s the excuse exploitation always uses. It puts the blame for the harm on the person who gets hurt. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Write your rules down

  1. 1

    Before the first session, say what you don’t do: haggling, free previews, bargains.

  2. 2

    Say plainly, ahead of time, when a sub mustn’t send, and that you’ll stop the session and refuse the send if he does.

  3. 3

    When a rule gets tested, point to it. Don’t argue.

  4. 4

    When a sub says money is short, pause the money part of the evening.

  5. 5

    When a sub is caught lying about another dom, block him instead of spending energy on it. A firm no ends it, and stop messaging when someone asks you to.

Saying no

Refuse once, calmly

  1. 1

    Say the boundary once, in one sentence.

  2. 2

    Don’t explain, justify or prove it.

  3. 3

    Wait before answering anything that comes with a deadline.

  4. 4

    If the pressure keeps going after the no, that’s your answer. Leave, block, keep the messages and tell one person you trust.

Name the lever

  1. Question 1 of 3A dom thanks you for a send in a lukewarm way and hints that you can obviously afford more. What is it?

    Answer. A sales line to push the amount up. The line praises and shames you in one breath. Set the amount before the evening.

  2. Question 2 of 3A dom says she respects your limits, then asks for a small test just past them. What do you do?

    Answer. Take the respect and ignore the next sentence. Words about limits don’t mean the nudge has stopped.

  3. Question 3 of 3A dom gives permission but says she’ll just be sad. What’s the cost?

    Answer. A permission that comes with guilt attached. Ask for a plain yes or no, and talk about the need underneath.

When the spending stops being a game

Some signs say a sub is deep in compulsion: spending hundreds in one day, paying several doms at once, weeks of chatting without calming down, deleting chats after each send. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Hiding the spending from a partner is a risk factor, and one author compares some cases to problem gambling. ReportedStated by a serious secondary source read in full.The Psychology of Financial Dominatrixes (blog post) (opens in a new tab) Accessed 3 November 2026. Compulsion needs slower handling and no extra asks. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A dom who earns from a sub can’t be his safety net. She can stop, listen, ask for nothing more and point him outward. Tapering his spending herself or moving him to a better dom is a conflict of interest, and it doesn’t help him. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. In a crisis, the help is outside: emergency services, a helpline, a person who’s physically nearby. Go to The spiral, Breaks that hold, When a sub is in trouble and Where to get help.
