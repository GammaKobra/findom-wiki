Step 1 of 6 Why “fake” is the wrong first question

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A fake dom wants your money or your accounts and gives you nothing true back.
  • The five signs: art that proves nothing, payment before any terms, a timer, odd payment routes, and answers that don’t answer.
  • Never give anyone a password, a login code or a remote session, whatever the reason.
  • A screenshot or a public tally proves nothing.
  • Your own limit protects you more than the dom you pick.

You will learn

  • What to do when a send goes to the wrong dom.

I’m a character too, so don’t trust me on looks either. Check what I ask for the same way you’d check any dom.

Proof that proves nothing

Text on a screen can be edited before it’s captured, so a screenshot of a chat or a payment proves very little. In this scene, doctored images get used to fake proof and to turn subs against a dom. If a screenshot says something about your dom, ask her directly before you leave. Keep your own receipts and messages from the start. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A public count of wins shows that a dom is trusted.

    Fact

    A running tally is advertising. All it shows is that someone wants you to see a tally. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A dom who posts proof of sends is safe.

    Fact

    Proof of sends can be staged, and you can’t tell the difference. In my experience it happens, so I give it no weight. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    A screenshot of a conversation proves what was said.

    Fact

    Anyone can edit the text on a screen before capturing it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Red flags

Moves to refuse

  • A stranger replies to your post and asks you to message them first.
  • A move off the platform to a private app, with cheap or free video as the bait.
  • A request to forward money, a file or content to someone else.

Counter. Don’t follow. Block the account and report it on the platform. The middleman carries the risk and the blame. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A dom who’s honest about money

A serious dom shows her terms before she asks for money: her limits, what she won’t do, prices in plain numbers and a visible way to stop. A dom who hides these has told you what she is. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Check yourself

  1. Question 1 of 3A dom asks for a one-time code “to prove you’re real”. What is it?

    Answer. A request for your keys. A code opens your account. A dom never needs one.

  2. Question 2 of 3A screenshot shows up that seems to show your dom with someone else. What do you do first?

    Answer. Ask her directly. A screenshot is easy to edit. Ask before you act.

  3. Question 3 of 3A stranger asks you to forward a file and some money to a third person. What is it?

    Answer. A request to be the middleman. You’d carry the risk and the blame.
