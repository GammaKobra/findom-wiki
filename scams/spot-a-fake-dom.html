In 30 seconds

  • A fake dom wants your money or your accounts and gives you nothing true in return.
  • The five signs: art that proves nothing, payment before any frame, a timer, odd payment routes, and answers that do not answer.
  • Never hand over a password, a login code or a remote session, whatever the reason.
  • A screenshot or a public tally proves nothing.
  • Your own cap protects you more than your choice of dom.

  • What to do when a send lands in the wrong place.

Every dom in this game is a character, me included, so a face cannot tell you who is fake. What a dom asks for can. Watch the requests: money before terms, a timer, a code, a move to a private app. I do not ask you to trust me. I ask you to check what any dom does, starting with me.

Never hand over keys

A request for a login, a one-time code or a remote-desktop sessionremote sessionA session in which the dom controls the sub’s computer through remote-desktop software. It is a red line of this wiki, because it hands over the screen, the accounts and the saved logins, and removes the sub’s own ability to interrupt. A US cyber-security agency advises people not to give control of their computers or phones to a stranger. A remote-access vendor advises users not to give device access to people they do not know or trust. If access was already given, close the app, cut the connection, change the passwords from another device and ask a person you trust or the service concerned for help. is the warning I see repeated most. It often arrives as proof of honesty or as the price of keeping the bond, which is when a person is least able to say no. A spoken promise to do no harm protects nothing. Proof that proves nothing

Text on a screen can be changed before it is captured, so a screenshot of a chat or a payment proves little. In this scene doctored images are used to fake proof and to turn subs against a dom. If a screenshot says something about your dom, ask her directly before you leave. Keep your own receipts and messages from the start. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Red flags

Moves to refuse

  • A stranger replies to your post and asks you to message first.
  • A move off the platform to a private app, with cheap or free video as the lure.
  • A request to forward money, a file or content to a third person.

Counter. Do not follow. Block the account and report it on the platform. A middleman carries the risk and the blame. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A dom who is honest about money

A dom who is serious shows her frame before she asks for money: her limits, what she refuses, plain prices and a visible way to stop. A dom who hides these has told you what she is. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Check yourself

  1. Question 1 of 3A dom asks for a one-time code “to prove you are real”. What is it?

    Answer. A request for your keys. A code opens your account. A dom never needs one.

  2. Question 2 of 3A screenshot arrives that seems to show your dom with someone else. What do you do first?

    Answer. Ask her directly. A screenshot is easy to alter. Ask before you act.

  3. Question 3 of 3A stranger asks you to forward a file and some money to a third person. What is it?

    Answer. A request to become the middleman. You would carry the risk and the blame.
