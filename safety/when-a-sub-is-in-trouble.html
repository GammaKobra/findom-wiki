Safety Chapter 5

When a sub is in trouble

Last checked 3 November 20267 minutes to read

A dom who is paid by a sub cannot be his safety net. This page says what she can do instead, what to do in a crisis, and which pressure moves to refuse.

VerifiedChecked against the original source.Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A dom who earns from a sub cannot be his safety net.
  • What she can do is simple: stop, listen, ask for nothing, and point outward.
  • Talk of self-harm is a crisis. Drop the persona and send him to emergency services, a helpline and someone near him.
  • When a sub says money is short, the money part stops for that evening.
  • Debts, rescues and “better doms” are conflicts of interest when the person paid runs them.

I am paid by the people who play with me, and that is why I say this plainly: a dom who earns from a sub cannot be his safety net. She can be a decent person in the moment. She can stop, listen, ask for nothing more and point him outward. The rest belongs to people who are not paid by his spending.

The rule: stop, listen, point outward

A dom who is paid by a subsubThe person who obeys and, in findom, usually spends within the game. Press also calls subs slaves, pets and paypigs. The scene counts as active only the subs who actually send, and in my experience most doms have very few. cannot be his safety net. She earns from his spending, so her advice about his spending comes from an interested party. Tapering a compulsive spender herself, managing his debts, or moving him to a better dom is a conflict of interest. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. What she can do is small, and it matters.

What a dom does when a sub is in trouble

  1. 1

    Stop the money part for the evening.

  2. 2

    Ask what is wrong, and listen. Pitch nothing.

  3. 3

    Answer kindly. He never has to apologise for how he feels.

  4. 4

    Ask for nothing more, today and while he is struggling.

  5. 5

    Point outward: to his own cap, to a person near him, and to an independent service in Where to get help.

VVivian’s field notes

My position is short. The one rule of the game is consent, a limit that is real is never crossed, and when someone is in trouble the dom stops. If a sub shows signs of compulsion, the session ends and she points him to his own cap and to real help.

A cap typed in the middle of a session is weak, and a cap is a ceiling, never a target. he set in a calm moment and keeps out of the moment’s reach, a person who knows, and an independent service. Red flags

Signs a sub may be in trouble

  • He sends a large amount in a short time, then vanishes.
  • He pays several doms at once.
  • He chats for weeks without calming down.
  • He says he is going downhill, or sees no future.
  • He keeps returning to a page to act on urges, and says he would have sent if the account were not empty. An empty account is luck, not a cure.

Counter. Stop the money part, ask, listen, and point outward. Do not read a calm week as a cure.

These signs come from watching the scene over time, and none of them proves anything alone. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What to say

When a sub says he regrets a send, a caring dom tells him to stop, says what she took, and moves to comfort without another ask. She asks how he feels and lets him talk. When he says he is going downhill and wants to continue another day, she accepts at once. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

If someone says he wants to hurt himself or to die

  1. 1

    Drop the persona and the argument. Write as a person, in plain words.

  2. 2

    Say you take it seriously, and ask directly whether he is safe now.

  3. 3

    Ask him to contact the emergency services or a helpline in his own country now, and to reach someone physically near him. Use the official list in Where to get help, never a number from memory.

  4. 4

    Stay calm and kind. Do not argue or bargain, and do not make him responsible for your feelings.

  5. 5

    Do not mention money, and do not use his distress or your own as leverage.

  6. 6

    If you believe the danger is immediate and you know where he is, contact the emergency services for that place. If you are in another country or do not know where he is, a call may not connect: use the platform’s self-harm report tool and look up that country’s police line on an official site.

  7. 7

    Send the message even if it is late for him or you think he may be asleep. A late hour is no reason to wait.

  8. 8

    Afterwards, tell someone you trust. Do not carry it alone.

Silence after such a message does not mean he is fine. A thank-you from the sub is not proof that he is safe either. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Debt and hardship

When a sub says money is short, the money part stops for that evening. Some of these claims are false and some are true, and the cost of being wrong falls on the sub. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Nobody can tell from a chat which kind she is reading.

What a decent dom does, and what she refuses
What they say or doThe counter
He says he can hardly pay for food this monthThe money part stops for the evening. She asks for nothing and does not test the claim.
He is in debtShe asks for nothing while he is in debt, does not manage or advise on the debt, and points to independent debt advice.
He insists it is fine and asks to send moreShe keeps the cap. Insistence is a reason to keep or tighten a limit, never to lift it.
He lies about moneyA lie may be born of compulsion or told to dodge a session. She cannot tell the two apart, so she keeps the money part stopped and keeps listening in both cases.
She is uneasy about how much he has sentShe stops the money part, asks how he is, and points to outside help.

These are field notes, not rules of law. Anyone who owes money to a lender needs independent advice, see Where to get help.

  • Myth

    Most subs who say they are broke are lying.

    Fact

    Some are and some are not, and the sub pays for her mistake. Do not ask for more when he says money is short. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I can help him by making him depend on a better dom.

    Fact

    That sends his money to the same circle and leaves his dependence in place. Call it redirection. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Someone else would drain him anyway, so it may as well be me.

    Fact

    That is the justification of exploitation. It moves the responsibility for harm onto the person who is already in trouble. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I am not the cause, because he thanked me.

    Fact

    That thought can protect your own peace. It becomes unsafe when it makes you stop trying to reach him. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

An offer of help to quit from the person who is paid is suspect, because she earns from his spending, so her advice is not neutral. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Independent help is the answer.

Quiet, quitting and pressure

When he goes quiet. A silent sub may be trying to quit, and a gap of a week or two can be normal. A caring dom sends at most one short check-in, with no invitation to return, and accepts that he may not answer. A sub who has quit gets nothing. Pressing does not make anyone admit what he wants to hide. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When he quits. Respect it. Do not chase him and do not message him again, and wish him a life he wants. A line such as “my door is always open” can double as a soft hook, so leave it out. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A friend outside the game. For a sub who feels unloved or valued only for his money, a friend elsewhere in the community who is reachable and consistent can help. A friend supports and does not replace professional help. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A goodbye that worries you

This is an invented exercise. A sub you know writes a message that says goodbye and hints that he may hurt himself. He does not answer when you reply.

For doms: look after yourself

VVivian’s field notes

Doms and makers burn out. The signs are pressure on yourself, dread each time you open the project, and a feeling that nothing works. The answer is to step back: go private for a while, stop posting and stop starting new things for a few days or a week, and keep talking to the people already in your life. You can come back later. Replying less often can protect you and can leave a sub alone at the worst time, so point him to help before you go quiet.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

One more rule for makers: never send hypnotic or trance-style content to someone who may be driving or operating anything, because it can cause an accident. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
