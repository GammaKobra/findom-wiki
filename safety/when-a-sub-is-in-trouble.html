Step 1 of 6 The rule: stop, listen, send him to real help

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • A dom who earns from a sub can’t be his safety net.
  • What she can do is simple: stop, listen, ask for nothing, and send him to help outside the game.
  • Talk of self-harm is a crisis. Drop the persona and send him to emergency services, a helpline and someone near him.
  • When a sub says money is short, the money part stops for that evening.
  • Debts, rescues and “better doms” are conflicts of interest when the person who’s paid runs them.

The rule: stop, listen, send him to real help

A dom who’s paid by a subsubThe person who obeys and, in findom, usually spends within the game. The press also calls subs slaves, pets and paypigs. The scene counts as active only the subs who actually send, and in my experience most doms have very few. can’t be his safety net. She earns from his spending, so her advice about his spending is never neutral. If she tapers a compulsive spender off herself, manages his debts or moves him to a better dom, that’s a conflict of interest. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. What she can do is small, and it matters.

What a dom does when a sub is in trouble

  1. 1

    Stop the money part for the evening.

  2. 2

    Ask what’s wrong, and listen. Don’t pitch anything.

  3. 3

    Answer kindly. He never has to apologise for how he feels.

  4. 4

    Ask for nothing more, today or while he’s struggling.

  5. 5

    Point him to his own limit, to a person near him, and to an independent service in Where to get help.

VVivian’s field notes

My position: consent is the one rule of the game, a real limit is never crossed, and when someone’s in trouble the dom stops. If a sub shows signs of compulsion, the session ends and she points him to his own limit and to real help.

Red flags

Signs a sub may be in trouble

  • He sends a large amount in a short time, then disappears.
  • He pays several doms at once.
  • He chats for weeks without calming down.
  • He says he’s going downhill, or sees no future.
  • He keeps coming back to a page to act on urges, and says he’d have sent if his account weren’t empty. An empty account is luck. It doesn’t cure anything.

Counter. Stop the money part, ask, listen, and point him to help outside the game. Don’t take a calm week for a cure.

These signs come from watching the scene over time, and none of them proves anything on its own. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

What to say

When a sub says he regrets a send, a caring dom tells him to stop, says what she took, and comforts him without asking for anything else. She asks how he feels and lets him talk. When he says he’s going downhill and wants to carry on another day, she agrees right away. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

If someone says he wants to hurt himself or to die

  1. 1

    Drop the persona and the argument. Write as a person, in plain words.

  2. 2

    Say you take it seriously, and ask him straight out whether he’s safe right now.

  3. 3

    Ask him to contact emergency services or a helpline in his own country now, and to reach someone physically near him. Use the official list in Where to get help, never a number from memory.

  4. 4

    Stay calm and kind. Don’t argue or bargain, and don’t make him responsible for your feelings.

  5. 5

    Don’t mention money, and don’t use his distress or your own as leverage.

  6. 6

    If you believe the danger is immediate and you know where he is, contact the emergency services for that place. If you’re in another country or don’t know where he is, a call may not connect: use the platform’s self-harm report tool and look up that country’s police line on an official site.

  7. 7

    Send the message now, even if it’s late for him or you think he may be asleep.

  8. 8

    Afterwards, tell someone you trust. Don’t carry it alone.

If he goes silent after a message like that, it doesn’t mean he’s fine. A thank-you from him doesn’t prove he’s safe either. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Debt and hardship

When a sub says money is short, the money part stops for that evening. Some of these claims are false and some are true, and if she guesses wrong, the sub is the one who pays for it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Nobody can tell from a chat which kind she’s reading.

What a decent dom does, and what she refuses
What they say or doThe counter
He says he can hardly pay for food this monthThe money part stops for the evening. She asks for nothing and doesn’t test the claim.
He’s in debtShe asks for nothing while he’s in debt, doesn’t manage the debt or advise on it, and points him to independent debt advice.
He insists it’s fine and asks to send moreShe keeps to his limit. If he insists, that’s a reason to keep or tighten the limit, never to lift it.
He lies about moneyHe may lie because of the compulsion, or to get out of a session. She can’t tell which, so either way she keeps the money part stopped and keeps listening.
She’s uneasy about how much he’s sentShe stops the money part, asks how he is, and points him to outside help.

These are field notes. They aren’t legal rules. Anyone who owes money to a lender needs independent advice: see Where to get help.

  • Myth

    Most subs who say they’re broke are lying.

    Fact

    Some are and some aren’t, and the sub pays for her mistake. Don’t ask for more when he says money is short. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I can help him by making him depend on a better dom.

    Fact

    That sends his money to the same circle and leaves his dependence where it was. Call it what it is: redirection. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    Someone else would drain him anyway, so it may as well be me.

    Fact

    That’s how exploitation justifies itself. It puts the responsibility for the harm on the person who’s already in trouble. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I’m not the cause, because he thanked me.

    Fact

    That thought can protect your own peace. It turns unsafe when it makes you stop trying to reach him. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When the person who’s paid offers to help him quit, treat that offer with suspicion. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Independent help is the answer.

Quiet, quitting and pressure

When he goes quiet. A silent sub may be trying to quit, and a gap of a week or two can be normal. A caring dom sends one short check-in at most, without inviting him back, and accepts that he may not answer. A sub who has quit gets no messages at all. Pushing won’t make anyone admit what he wants to hide. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When he quits. Respect it. Don’t chase him, don’t message him again, and wish him the life he wants. A line like “my door is always open” can also work as a soft hook, so leave it out. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A friend outside the game. If a sub feels unloved, or valued only for his money, a friend elsewhere in the community who’s easy to reach and steady can help. A friend is support, and doesn’t replace professional help. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A goodbye that worries you

This is an invented exercise. A sub you know writes a message that says goodbye and hints that he may hurt himself. He doesn’t answer when you reply.

For doms: look after yourself

VVivian’s field notes

Doms and makers burn out. The signs: you put pressure on yourself, you dread opening the project, and nothing seems to work. Step back. Go private for a while, stop posting and stop starting new things for a few days or a week, and keep talking to the people already in your life. You can come back later. Replying less often can protect you, but it can also leave a sub alone at the worst time, so point him to help before you go quiet.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

One more rule for makers: never send hypnotic or trance-style content to someone who may be driving or operating anything, because it can cause an accident. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.
