Step 1 of 5 The first hour

This chapter is not advice for your situation.

In 30 seconds

  • One send isn’t the end of anything. What you do in the next hour matters more than the send.
  • Stop, keep everything, wait a day, and tell one person you trust.
  • Never hand over a password, a login code or control of a device to prove anything.
  • Don’t send again to get a send back. Nobody can promise you a refund.
  • If rent, food or bills are at risk, stop and ask for independent help.

The first hour

It happens a lot, and what you do next matters more than the sendsendA payment from a sub to a dom, often by buying a wishlist item. Informal forms are sends and sendies. The press calls a payment made without a session a silent tribute. A send buys nothing, and in practice you can’t get it refunded. itself. The usual cycle goes like this: you want to pay, you send, the guilt hits hard, you hide it, and when things feel good again you do it again. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. The guilt is real. Hiding it is what keeps the cycle turning. See The spiral.

If you already sent

  1. 1

    Stop sending, even to “unlock a refund”.

  2. 2

    Keep the messages and the payment receipts.

  3. 3

    Report the account on the platform where you met.

  4. 4

    Tell someone you trust what happened.

Step three is for an account you think may be fake. If you just regret a send to a real dom, skip it and do the other three. To check whether an account is fake, read Spot a fake dom.

Wait one day. Some people feel low after a send, and that’s a bad time to decide anything about money, the dom or the account. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Don’t make any new decision until you’ve slept and eaten.

Tell one person. Hiding the send adds a second problem on top of the first, and telling someone you trust soon after the first send is better than waiting. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. One blog post on findom names hiding the spending from a partner as a risk factor. One sentence is enough: I sent money I shouldn’t have, and I want to stop. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Pick someone near you who doesn’t earn from your spending.

What not to do

  • Myth

    Deleting the whole conversation will help me move on.

    Fact

    Deleting the chat feeds the loop and wipes what you may need for a report or for help. Deleting your account on the platform you send from often doesn’t hold either, because other accounts and other paid forms of the same play are still there. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    One last send will get my money back.

    Fact

    A send made to fix a send is just the same send again. A refund offered after one more payment, often called processing or insurance, is how a fake account keeps going. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    It’s better to give to this dom than to a stranger.

    Fact

    That sentence is how the next send gets permission. It’s an excuse, and you can drop it. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I should prove I’m sincere by handing over access.

    Fact

    You never prove sincerity with a password or a code. A real dom needs no proof from you. If she asks to see a send, the only safe thing to show is a confirmation with the names, balance and numbers hidden. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

  • Myth

    I owe what she says I owe.

    Fact

    A round figure she says you owe after one session is only framing. Inside the game, there’s no debt you didn’t agree to beforehand. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

If you need to cut contact, muting or blocking is up to you. Save the messages first. A break that holds takes away the means, covers the substitutes and involves someone who’s told the truth. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Set a limitlimitA spending limit picked by the person who pays, while calm, before the session. It’s held outside your own head, in a server that enforces it, a bank limit or a trusted person, and it only goes up after a cooldown. A limit typed in the middle of a session is weak, and a limit is a ceiling, never a target. with a reset day and keep your bank’s confirmations on. See Breaks that hold.

If you gave access or a code

When someone asks for your password, a login code sent to your phone, or control of your device, that’s how accounts get taken over. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Security guidance from a government agency and from a remote-access vendor says not to give control of a device to a stranger or to anyone you don’t know or trust. Secure what you shared

  1. 1

    Change the password of the account you shared, and of any other account that used the same password. Do it from your own device, never from a link someone sent you.

  2. 2

    Sign out of all other sessions and devices in the account’s security settings, then turn on two-step login.

  3. 3

    If you gave a login code, assume someone else is already in the account until you’ve checked it.

  4. 4

    Remove saved payment methods from that account. If someone controlled your computer, remove the remote tool and don’t use the device for banking until it’s been checked.

  5. 5

    If a card or account number was shared, tell your bank and ask what it can do.

  6. 6

    Report the request to the platform where you met.

Money

Whether a send can come back depends on how it was made, the platform and the country. This wiki couldn’t find a public rule for tributestributeA payment offered to a dom as homage. A domme interviewed by the press describes tribute as unconditional rather than a fee. A tribute buys nothing, and nobody can enforce a promise made in a scene. as of 2026-11-03. UnverifiedPlausible, not proven yet. Don’t count on a refund. A tribute is a gift, and in practice tributes don’t get returned just because the sender changed his mind. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

Don’t pay anyone who promises to get your money back. If the account that took the money offers a rescue, like a recovery fee, it’s a trap. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. What you can do is ask your bank or the payment platform, calmly and soon, what options there are and how long you have.

If you can’t cover rent, food or bills, stop sending, tell someone, and get independent debt advice, never from the person who was paid. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. One press article quotes a former participant who says he’d seen men sell cars, go into debt and take out second jobs. What a decent dom does when you say stop

VVivian’s field notes

When someone tells a dom he regrets a send, she should tell him to stop, say plainly what she took, ask how he is and ask for nothing more. That’s what you can expect from a decent dom. If she answers your regret with another offer, she’s thinking about her income, and now you know what you needed to know.

Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

A plain goodbye is the model. One that leaves the door open can also work as a soft hook to pull you back. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study. Wait before you answer it.

If the money and the feelings have got mixed up and you feel like you’re buying affection, remember that affection can’t be bought. What you chose to pay for is the play and the attention, and a gift doesn’t buy a promise, a rank or a reply. A decent dom is just as warm when you send a little as when you send a lot. Vivian’s field notesFirst-hand experience, not a study.

When someone presses you after a send
What they say or doThe counter
Confess everything by tonight or it gets worseAsk for a pause. A deadline that takes away your choice is pressure, whoever sets it.
Write down when, why and how much, in fullSay only what you choose to say. Being forced to write it all down isn’t an honest conversation.
Hand over your accounts and delete nothingNever hand over access. See above.
Answer now or all contact endsAsk for the talk to wait until both of you are rested and sober, and bring in a person you trust.

Someone who’s tired or drinking can’t answer or listen properly, so hard talks should wait. A partner who finds out deserves a calm talk.
